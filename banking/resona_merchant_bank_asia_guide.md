## 1. Company Overview

### 1.1 The Incorporation — 19 May 1981, as ASEAN Finance Corporation (Verified)

Resona Merchant Bank Asia Limited began life on **19 May 1981** — 45 years before this guide's research date — under a very different name: **ASEAN Finance Corporation** ("AFC"). The incorporation date is one of the best-corroborated facts in this profile: the ACRA-derived aggregators (companies.sg, recordowl.com, sgbizdb.com) all report **19 May 1981**; Resona Holdings' own 30 January 2017 notice lists AFC's "Date Established" as **May 19, 1981**; and Resona Holdings' Major Group Companies page (as of 1 April 2026) repeats the same date for RMBA. Three independent source families agree — that is the definition of verified in this series.

The company is a **Local Company** (Singapore-incorporated, as opposed to a foreign-company branch) — and note that this distinguishes it from the more common shape of foreign-bank presence in Singapore, where a foreign bank typically operates through a *branch* (e.g. the Japanese megabanks' wholesale branches — see §6.3). RMBA is a **Singapore-incorporated subsidiary**: a local public company that happens to be owned by a Japanese banking group. That structural choice — subsidiary, not branch — is central to its merchant-bank licensing story (§5).

### 1.2 The UEN — 198102227E (Verified)

The Unique Entity Number **198102227E** was issued by ACRA with incorporation (the "1981" prefix encodes the incorporation year; the trailing "E" is the check letter). The UEN is consistent across the MAS Financial Institutions Directory and every ACRA-derived aggregator consulted — no discrepancy. The same UEN appears in the MAS FID's licence record, which is how the registry record and the licence record are cross-confirmed: **the company holding the merchant-bank designation and the company registered with ACRA are one and the same entity**.

### 1.3 The Entity Type — Public Company Limited by Shares (Verified)

RMBA is registered as a **Public Company Limited by Shares** — an *unlisted* public company (Resona's 2017 notice: "Listed/Unlisted: Unlisted"). In Singapore's company-law vocabulary this does not mean publicly traded; it means the company's shares are not subject to the transfer restrictions of a private company (which caps members at 50 and restricts share transfers). The label matters for two reasons: (1) it is the standard shell for a **MAS-licensed financial institution** — ACRA guidance and MAS practice favour the public-company form for licensed/approved entities, and (2) it squares with the ownership history — a company held by corporate shareholders (AFC's ASEAN holding vehicles pre-2017, then Resona Bank) rather than a handful of individuals. The current shareholder base is a single corporate parent: Resona Bank, Limited (100% since 31 July 2017 — §2.3).

### 1.4 The Registered Office — 8 Marina View, #32-03, Asia Square Tower 1 (Verified)

The registered office is **8 Marina View, #32-03, Asia Square Tower 1, Singapore 018960** — the 43-storey first tower of the Marina Bay financial district's Asia Square complex, a few hundred metres from the MAS building. The address is consistent across the ACRA-derived aggregators *and* the MAS Financial Institutions Directory, which lists the same address with the same unit number — the registered office and the regulated place of business coincide. (Pre-2017, the company was registered at **180 Cecil Street, #17-01, Bangkok Bank Building, Singapore 069546** — the AFC era's home, per Resona's 2017 notice; the move to Marina Bay post-dates the Resona acquisition ⚠ exact move date not verified.)

### 1.5 The Paid-Up Capital — S$194,845,190 (and the S$68,845,190 Aggregator Echo) (Verified with flag)

The current paid-up capital is **S$194,845,190**, represented by **194,845,190 ordinary shares** (companies.sg shows the amount; RecordOwl's shareholding table shows the same number as ordinary-share quantity). This is a large capital base for a merchant bank — roughly S$195 million of paid-up capital behind a thin-footprint wholesale business, which is the kind of number that speaks to the parent group capitalising its regional vehicle generously (and to the paid-up requirements MAS attaches to approved financial institutions).

⚠ **The aggregator echo, named rather than smoothed:** RecordOwl's *prose* still states paid-up capital of **S$68,845,190** — which is not a scrape error but the **pre-acquisition figure**: Resona's 30 January 2017 notice lists AFC's "Issued Paid-up Shares" as **SGD 68,845,190** (JPY 5,537 million) as of end-December 2015, and the same notice records Resona Bank's acquisition of **100,000,000 shares** (100% of voting rights) through a Singapore court-approved scheme. The S$194.8 million current figure therefore reflects post-2017 capital increases (the 100 million shares Resona subscribed plus subsequent issuance ⚠ the exact split between the 2017 scheme and later increases is not publicly itemised). RecordOwl appears to have carried the pre-acquisition number into its summary text while its structured share table shows the current one. Both figures are real; they describe different dates.

## 2. The Lineage — from ASEAN Finance Corporation to the Resona Group

This is the section where the thin-footprint method earns its keep. The entity now called Resona Merchant Bank Asia Limited is **not** a Japanese bank that opened a Singapore office; it is a **1981-vintage ASEAN merchant bank that a Japanese group bought outright in 2017 and renamed**. The lineage is documented in the parent group's own words, so it can be told with unusual confidence.

### 2.1 The 1981 Founding — an ASEAN Co-operative Venture (Verified with secondary sources)

Resona's 30 January 2017 notice describes the acquired company in one sentence that anchors the whole story: **"AFC is a financial institution established in 1981 by major financial institutions in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and the Philippines for the purpose of infrastructure development in the Region; it possesses an established customer base and a wealth of local information in the Region."**

That is the parent's own characterisation, and the academic record agrees:

- **Schulze, "The ASEAN Finance Corporation"** (JSTOR 25770188): AFC was **established in 1981 as a co-operative venture of commercial banks in ASEAN**, with objectives to **finance ASEAN industrial co-operative projects and to provide venture capital**; its performance through 1985 was characterised as poor — excess liquidity, slow growth (the early years were underwhelming; the entity survived and steadied).
- **UNIDO study, "Asean Finance Corporation — Prospects and Challenge"**: the initial **paid-up capital was set at S$100 million, divided equally among the five members**, offered to qualified ASEAN investors; headquarters in **Singapore**; no branches contemplated in ASEAN countries, with business channelled through member banks.
- **UIA Yearbook profile**: founded 1981, Singapore; aims — "mobilize financial resources to finance projects and assist in promotion of industrialization and overall economic development in the ASEAN region."

## 3. The Group Context — Resona Holdings and the Resona Group

### 3.1 The Daiwa/Asahi Lineage — 1918 to 2003 (Verified with secondary sources)

The parent group's story is long and well-documented (Wikipedia's Resona Holdings article — flagged as secondary but heavily cited):

- **1918:** founded as **Osaka Nomura Bank**, the financing arm of the Nomura zaibatsu; the securities arm split off in 1925 to become today's Nomura Securities; renamed **Nomura Bank** 1927; renamed **The Daiwa Bank** 1948 — one of the few postwar Japanese majors offering both banking and trust services.
- **1995:** the **Iguchi scandal** — a New York Daiwa bond trader lost **US$1.1 billion** speculating and hid the losses; the bank was indicted, ordered out of the US market, and talks of a rescue merger with Sumitomo Bank collapsed. Daiwa closed its US operations in 1996 and **pulled out of overseas banking entirely in 1998** — the episode that explains why the group's Singapore story later had to be rebuilt via acquisition.
- **The Asahi side:** Japan Saving Bank (a 1945 nine-bank consolidation) → Kyowa Bank (1948) → **Kyowa Saitama Bank** (1991, after merging with Saitama Bank) → **Asahi Bank** (1992). Asahi's 2000–2001 flirtation with the Sanwa/Tokai three-way merger fell through (Sanwa + Tokai went on to form UFJ).
- **December 2001:** Daiwa forms **Daiwa Bank Holdings** (parent of Daiwa, Kinki Osaka Bank, Nara Bank) and announces the **share-swap acquisition of Asahi Bank**, forming Japan's fifth-largest banking group.
- **1 October 2002:** renamed **Resona Holdings, Inc.**; **1 March 2003:** Daiwa + Asahi consolidated into **Resona Bank**, with 100 Asahi branches in Saitama (plus three Tokyo branches) hived into **Saitama Resona Bank**.

### 3.2 The 2003 Capital Crisis and the ¥1.96 Trillion Bailout (Verified with secondary sources)

The group's near-death experience is central to understanding its present character. In early 2003, Resona's capital adequacy ratio fell below the legal floor once deferred tax assets were excluded — its auditor refused to certify the financials, and the bank was effectively insolvent. On **17 May 2003** the Japanese government injected **¥1.96 trillion** of public funds into the group through Resona Bank — at the time the largest bailout in Japanese financial history — leaving the state with **68.25% of voting rights** (effectively nationalised), the existing management sacked, and **Eiji Hosoya** installed to lead the revival. The group returned to profit in 2004 (¥386 billion) and announced a plan to repay ¥868 billion of the ~¥3 trillion in public funds received; the government stake was progressively sold down in the following decade ⚠ (exact exit date not verified here). The episode left the group with a conservative, regional, deposit-funded character — and, one can reasonably observe, a preference for *subsidiary* acquisitions over risky organic overseas expansion (the 2017 AFC purchase fits that mould: small, sound, fully-owned, and cheap relative to a banking licence).

### 5.2 What Merchant-Bank Status Means Under the MAS Act (Verified context)

Singapore's merchant banks are a **distinct regulatory species** from banks (see [banks_in_singapore_guide.md](banks_in_singapore_guide.md) §5.4 and §9 for the full taxonomy): they are **approved by MAS under the MAS Act** (section 28/55 approval — the "approved merchant bank" regime) rather than **licensed under the Banking Act**. Consequences that matter for this profile:

- **No deposit-taking from the public** — merchant banks may not take deposits from the public; they fund wholesale (interbank, parent, capital markets). This is why the *paid-up capital* (S$194.8M) rather than a deposit base is the balance-sheet anchor.
- **MAS Act discipline** — approval carries conditions, MAS supervision, and the full AML/CFT and prudential apparatus, but the entity is *not* a licensed bank and cannot hold itself out as one.
- **The species' original purpose** — the merchant-bank class was built for corporate finance, syndicated lending, capital-markets activity and (later) private banking; it is the natural licence for a wholesale finance/advisory house like RMBA, and the natural acquisition target for a foreign group wanting a regulated SG vehicle without a banking-licence application.
- **The ~45-merchant-bank population** ⚠ — banks_in_singapore_guide.md estimates ~45 merchant banks in the SG market (approximate MAS-statistics order of magnitude); RMBA is one of them, and one of the few with an explicitly *Japanese-group* parentage.

### 5.3 The Exempt CMS Scope — What RMBA May Do Without a CMS Licence (Verified)

Under the **Securities and Futures Act (SFA)**, carrying on business in regulated activities requires a Capital Markets Services (CMS) licence — unless exempt. Merchant banks approved under the MAS Act are among the classes exempted from CMS licensing for specified activities; the FID records the scope of RMBA's exemption precisely:

- **Dealing in Capital Markets Products — Securities** (i.e., dealing in shares, debentures, collective-investment units, etc.);
- **Dealing in Capital Markets Products — Over-The-Counter Derivatives Contracts** (OTC derivatives dealing);
- **Advising on Corporate Finance** (the SFA's regulated activity of advising on corporate finance — the licence category that underwrites M&A advisory and capital-raising advice).

These three items map one-to-one onto the 2017 mandate (lending + M&A advisory + capital-markets activity) and onto RecordOwl's description of the firm as a "Corporate Finance Adviser and Securities Firm regulated by MAS under the SFA" ⚠ (aggregator commentary, consistent with the FID). Notably absent from the exemption scope: **fund management**, **real-estate investment advice**, **custody** — the activities RMBA does *not* hold itself out to do. The Exempt CMS status also means RMBA is *not* a CMS licensee — a distinction KYC systems must handle correctly (a counterparty search against the CMS register would return nothing; the FID is the right register, §7).

## 6. The Market Context — SG Merchant Banks and the Japanese Presence

### 6.1 The Merchant-Bank Species in Singapore (Verified context)

Section 5.2 covered the legal shape; the *market* shape comes from [banks_in_singapore_guide.md](banks_in_singapore_guide.md): Singapore hosts roughly **160 commercial banks and ~45 merchant banks** ⚠ (approximate MAS statistics) — a merchant-bank layer that includes the private-banking arms of global groups (Bank of Singapore — OCBC's private bank — is the best-known local example ⚠-✅) and the wholesale finance houses. Merchant banks occupy the niche between banks and securities firms: corporate finance, syndicated lending, private banking, treasury — funded wholesale, regulated under the MAS Act. RMBA is one of the older registrations in the class (1981) but one of the newer *owners* (2017).

### 6.2 RMBA's Merchant-Bank Peers (Verified context)

The companies.sg "same principal activity" listing (August 2026 snapshot) shows the merchant-bank peer set in various states of life: **Aareal Bank Asia Limited** (live — the German property-finance group's SG merchant bank), **Asian American Merchant Bank Limited** (dissolved — members' voluntary winding up), **Armco Pacific Limited** (dissolved), **ANZ Singapore Limited** (dissolved), **AIBMB Ltd** (dissolved), **Antwerp Diamond Bank Asia Pacific Ltd** (dissolved), **AFC Merchant Bank** (the old-format registration ⚠ — ceased registration; a separate registration from RMBA's own UEN, likely the AFC era's operational arm — relationship to 198102227E not established), plus the live bank-branch entries (Axis Bank Singapore branch, ANZ's private-banking remnants). The pattern: **the SG merchant-bank class is shrinking** — most of the 1980s–90s cohort has wound up — and the survivors are either group-captive wholesale vehicles (RMBA, Aareal) or private-banking franchises. RMBA's 45-year survival, with a 2017 ownership refresh, is unusual in the class.

### 6.3 The Japanese-Bank Layer — Megabanks vs the Regional-Group Vehicle (Verified context)

The Japanese presence in Singapore splits into two very different shapes ([banks_in_singapore_guide.md](banks_in_singapore_guide.md) names the Japanese majors among the wholesale tier; [smbc_software_systems_guide.md](smbc_software_systems_guide.md) profiles one of them in depth):

- **The megabanks — MUFG, SMBC, Mizuho:** full wholesale *bank branches* (and their securities/trust affiliates), among the largest foreign-bank operations in Singapore — the "JPMorgan/MUFG pattern" of the banks guide's §6. They run regional booking, huge balance sheets, and full markets/trade franchises.
- **The regional groups — Resona and peers:** a merchant-bank *subsidiary* (RMBA) plus rep offices, with a fraction of the megabank footprint. The contrast is structural: branch + Banking Act licence + MAS-supervised balance sheet vs subsidiary + MAS Act approval + wholesale-only scope.
- **Why the merchant-bank route for a regional group:** a regional group (deposit-funded, SME-centric, conservative post-2003 — §3.2) does not need a global markets platform; it needs a *regulated conduit* for its Japanese clients' ASEAN business — lending, advisory, introductions. The 2017 AFC purchase bought that conduit for the price of a small merchant bank instead of the cost and scrutiny of a banking-licence application. The strategy is visible in the group's own words: RMBA as "one of the key Resona Group's overseas offices" serving mainly-SME Japanese clients.

### 6.4 Why the Merchant-Bank Vehicle Suits a Regional Group (Verified context)

Three reasons the structure fits, drawn from the verified record:

1. **Speed and certainty** — acquisition of an existing MAS-approved merchant bank (2017: board resolution January, completion July) versus a de-novo licence application measured in years.
2. **Scope matched to need** — the exempt-CMS scope (securities dealing, OTC derivatives, corporate-finance advisory) covers exactly the lending/advisory/M&A mandate, without the compliance estate of a full bank.
3. **Ownership cleanliness** — a wholly-owned Singapore-incorporated subsidiary (Public Company Limited by Shares) is a clean vehicle for capital (S$194.8M paid-up), for regulatory reporting, and for the group's consolidated accounts.

### 7.6 The Lessons

1. **Thin footprint ≠ thin diligence.** The absence of a website and standalone financials made the onboarding *harder to shortcut*, not easier — the verified record (FID + registry + parent notices) turned out to be unusually complete once the right sources were found.
2. **The acquisition notices are the canonical documents.** For entities acquired by disclosure-heavy parents, the acquisition paperwork (here: Resona's 30 Jan and 28 Jul 2017 notices) often contains better data than the registry — shareholders, audited financials, mandate, and the rename date, all in one place.
3. **Register selection matters.** RMBA is exempt from CMS licensing; a naive CMS-licence search returns "not found" and could wrongly fail the counterparty. The MAS FID is the correct register for merchant banks.
4. **Aggregator discrepancies are data, not noise.** The S$68.8M vs S$194.8M paid-up figures and the "242 shareholders" row are *explainable* (pre-acquisition vs current; share-row misread) — the diligence discipline is to explain, not to smooth.
5. **The merchant-bank vehicle is a deliberate structural choice** — for a regional Japanese group, an acquired, wholly-owned, MAS-approved merchant bank is the efficient Singapore conduit, and any correspondent relationship should be sized to that reality, not to a megabank branch's reality.

## 8. The Summary — Resona Merchant Bank Asia in One Page

**Resona Merchant Bank Asia Limited** (UEN 198102227E) is Singapore's quietest Japanese-owned bank: a **MAS-approved merchant bank** — with exempt-CMS scope for dealing in securities and OTC derivatives and for corporate-finance advisory — wholly owned by **Resona Bank, Limited**, the core subsidiary of **Resona Holdings, Inc.** (TYO: 8308), Japan's Osaka-rooted regional-banking group.

Its story is a two-act structure. **Act one (1981–2017): ASEAN Finance Corporation.** Incorporated on 19 May 1981 as a co-operative venture of the ASEAN-5's financial institutions, to finance regional industrial projects and venture capital; a small, steady, dividend-paying merchant bank at 180 Cecil Street (phone +65 6224 7155 — still RMBA's number); owned by ASEAN holding vehicles (Asfinco Singapore, AFC Holdings Malaysia, PT Sarana Bersama Pengembangan Indonesia). **Act two (2017–): the Resona era.** Resona Bank resolved on 30 January 2017 to buy 100% of AFC (completing 31 July 2017), renamed it Resona Merchant Bank Asia Limited on the acquisition date, seconded personnel, and re-pointed the platform at its actual purpose: **loans, consulting, and M&A advisory for Japanese SMEs expanding into ASEAN and India** — "one of the key Resona Group's overseas offices", still described in 2026 as "financing business and consulting services".

The present shape: **S$194.8M paid-up capital**, registered at **8 Marina View #32-03, Asia Square Tower 1** (Singapore 018960), audited by **Deloitte & Touche LLP**, CEO **Yohei Ueba** / Deputy CEO **Masatoshi Shiomi** (MAS FID), **no website**, a handful of public mentions — a Sep 2024 Straits Times report naming it a creditor bank (represented by Selvam LLC) in the Yap Shiaw Wei bankruptcy action, mid-2026 job ads for an SVP of Structured Finance, and a Dec 2022 hosting of the group's Singapore customer event. The parent group behind it carries the full Daiwa/Asahi lineage (1918 Osaka Nomura Bank → 2003 merger → the ¥1.96 trillion bailout of May 2003 → the Hosoya revival) and a Kansai reshuffle that ended with Kansai Mirai Bank and Minato Bank as separate direct subsidiaries after the April 2024 absorption of the Kansai Mirai intermediate holding company.

For a counterparty, the one-line summary is: **a real, regulated, well-capitalised, deliberately small merchant bank whose honesty-to-footprint ratio is high** — every material fact about it is verifiable, provided you know that the verification lives in the MAS FID, the ACRA record, and the parent group's 2017 acquisition notices, *not* on a website that does not exist. *The quiet wholesale house.*

## 9. Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ACRA** | Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority — Singapore's company registrar; issues UENs and maintains the corporate register |
| **AFC** | ASEAN Finance Corporation — the entity's name from incorporation (1981) until the 2017 Resona acquisition and rename |
| **CMS licence** | Capital Markets Services licence under the Securities and Futures Act (SFA) — required for regulated activities unless exempt |
| **DBU** | Domestic Banking Unit — the older SSIC/regulatory label for a merchant bank's domestic (SGD) banking business; RMBA's pre-Aug-2024 activity code |
| **Exempt CMS Entity** | A MAS-regulated entity exempt from CMS licensing for specified activities — here: dealing in securities and OTC derivatives, and corporate-finance advisory |
| **FID** | MAS Financial Institutions Directory — the authoritative register of MAS-regulated institutions and their licences |
| **Kansai Mirai Bank / Minato Bank** | Resona Holdings' Kansai regional banks — separate subsidiaries since the April 2024 absorption of their intermediate holding company |
| **MAS Act** | Monetary Authority of Singapore Act — the statute under which merchant banks are approved (vs the Banking Act for licensed banks) |
| **Merchant bank** | A MAS-approved wholesale financial institution: no public deposit-taking; corporate finance, syndicated lending, private banking, treasury |
| **RMBA** | Resona Merchant Bank Asia Limited — this guide's subject |
| **SFA** | Securities and Futures Act — the statute governing capital-markets activities and CMS licensing |
| **SSIC** | Singapore Standard Industrial Classification — the activity-code taxonomy used in the ACRA register |
| **UEN** | Unique Entity Number — Singapore's business-entity identifier (here: 198102227E) |
