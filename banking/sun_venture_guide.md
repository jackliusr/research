## 1. The Overview

### 1.1 The Short Answer

**Which Sun Venture?** The name is used by unrelated firms in at least three jurisdictions, and — unusually — by the subject's *own* struck-off legacy entities, so the identity guardrail comes first. This guide profiles exactly one subject: **Sun Venture, the Singapore-headquartered, privately held multi-asset investment firm that operates sunventure.com**, describes itself as "a dynamic, privately held investment firm with a passion for long-term value", and states on its firm page that it was **"Founded in 2006"** as "a long-term investor with a methodical and disciplined approach to investing" that invests "across public and private markets". ✅ (sunventure.com, extracted September 2026; LinkedIn company page, extracted September 2026.)

The subject is anchored in the public registries by a **group of Singapore entities that share one registered address** — 71 Robinson Road #04-01, 71 Robinson, Singapore 068895 — and, decisively, by a **shared naming convention that ties the whole family together**:

- **SUN VENTURE PTE. LTD.** — UEN **201114838M**, incorporated **22 June 2011**, Live, SSIC 66309 (*Fund management activities n.e.c.*), former names **LEGEND CORPORATE MANAGEMENT** and **SV MANAGEMENT SERVICES**. ✅ (ACRA-derived public snapshots)
- **SUN VENTURE DEVELOPMENTS PTE. LTD.** — UEN **200804868M**, incorporated **11 March 2008**, Live, SSIC 68101 (*Property investments & development*), former name **DB & B DEVELOPMENTS**. ✅
- **SUN VENTURE REAL ESTATE (SINGAPORE) PTE. LTD.** — UEN **202226362M**, incorporated **28 July 2022**, Live, SSIC 64202 (*Holding companies of firms engaged in non-financial and insurance activities*). ✅
- Deal and geography SPVs: **SV ROBINSON PTE. LTD.** (UEN 201918338N, incorporated 7 June 2019 — the 71 Robinson Road acquirer), **SV PETIT PTE. LTD.** (202106583W), **SV CAMPANULA PTE. LTD.** (202116939R), **SVRE UK PTE. LTD.** (202226640H) and **SVRE AUSTRALIA PTE. LTD.** (202226379C). ✅
- And an **Isle of Man corporate arm**: **SUN VENTURE ASSET MANAGEMENT (ISLE OF MAN) LIMITED** (company 017723V, previously **LEGEND ASSET MANAGEMENT (ISLE OF MAN) LIMITED**) and **SUN VENTURE GLOBAL FUNDS (ISLE OF MAN) PCC LIMITED** (company 017724V, previously **LEGEND GLOBAL OPPORTUNITIES LIMITED**, dissolved 21 April 2023). ✅/⚠ (GLEIF/LEI records and IoM registry aggregators)

That the Singapore fund-management company's former name was **LEGEND CORPORATE MANAGEMENT**, and that the Isle of Man entities were formerly **LEGEND ASSET MANAGEMENT** and **LEGEND GLOBAL OPPORTUNITIES**, is the single most useful identity fact in this guide: it ties the Sun Venture brand directly to **Legend Investment Holdings**, the Hong Kong vehicle which Chairman Ricky Au says he incorporated in 2006 "to formally consolidate his investments under a structured framework", and which the law firm Eversheds Harry Elias named in 2019 as the group of which "Sun Venture Group ... is part". ✅ (site bio; Eversheds Harry Elias client note, 1 August 2019; GLEIF/LEI records.)

In one line each, the answers a banker most often wants: **Who are they?** A privately held, family-office-rooted Singapore multi-asset investment firm — real estate, public markets, private equity and fund of funds — controlled through a chairman-founder structure rather than a marketed fund franchise. **Are they regulated by MAS?** No entity under the Sun Venture name appears in the MAS Financial Institutions Directory — a verified negative as at the query date (§7.1), with the caveat that "no licence found" is not "no licence needed". **How big?** Not disclosed. This is the guide's most repeated ⚠: **no AUM figure exists in any public source** — not on the firm's site, not in a regulatory filing, not in the trade press (§6.1). What is public is the *deal record* (roughly S$655 million for 71 Robinson Road in Singapore, about £874 million across four London assets, A$148 million in Sydney) and a self-reported office in Douglas, Isle of Man. **Where is the money from?** The firm says capital is its own — "investing with our own capital enables us to build long-term sustainable businesses" — and Preqin classifies Sun Venture as **"a single-family office based in Singapore"** ⚠ (Preqin profile; site private-equity page).

For a bank, Sun Venture matters on four fronts: as a **corporate-client archetype** (a Singapore-headquartered multi-asset principal investor with a Singapore holding company, a Singapore fund-management company, project SPVs in two jurisdictions and an Isle of Man corporate office — the worked example in §11), as an **identity and screening case study** (the "Sun Venture" name produces false-positive hits from legacy group entities, unrelated Singapore namesakes and at least one US LLC, so the CDD file must document the disambiguation — §2.4 and §11.2), as a **regulatory-boundary reference** (an investment firm carrying an SSIC *fund management* code yet appearing nowhere on the MAS register — showing a bank how to assess an unlicensed-but-professional investor without assuming exemption, §7.3), and as a **worked-example generator** for this repository's Cymbal Bank persona conventions (§11).

### 1.3 Why This Matters to a Bank

A Sun-Venture-style client is a **core private-markets corporate-client type** that Singapore commercial banks meet regularly, and it is a *harder* case than it looks. The group is professional (a named chairman-founder, a full C-suite roster with verifiable prior employers, a decade and a half of dated transactions), it is materially sized (four London assets, a Singapore CBD tower, a Sydney tower, a hotel), and yet it publishes **no AUM, no fund names, no investor list, no financial statements** — presenting itself, in the firm's own words, as "a privately held firm that invests across multiple asset classes with the aim of maximising shareholder value". The banking questions are the ones §11 works through: How do you onboard a principal-investment group whose registered office is *inside the building it owns*, whose Singapore fund-management entity carries an SSIC fund-management code but no MAS licence, and whose name collides with struck-off legacy entities of its own group and with unrelated namesakes? How do you finance a Singapore property SPV (SV Robinson-style) when the sponsor's balance sheet is private? How do you handle treasury and FX across SGD, GBP, AUD, USD and Hong Kong dollars without a group cash-flow statement? And how do you set a periodic-review cycle for a client that has no regulator to do it for you? These are the questions the worked example in §11 answers, following the Cymbal Bank persona conventions of the repository.

### 4.2 The Business Model — A Multi-Asset House With a Family-Office Core

Stripping the marketing language, the model reads as a **permanent-capital, principal-investment house organised into three capability silos plus a balance-sheet core** (analysis in the guide's own words, anchored to the firm's own disclosures ✅/⚠):

- **The balance sheet is the fund.** Unlike a closed-end manager, Sun Venture does not appear to raise pooled third-party vehicles — the site's language is "our own capital", "maximising shareholder value", "long-term investment success" ⚠. That places the firm structurally closer to the repository's **family-office / principal-investor** archetype than to the GP/LP model analysed in the [Private Equity Guide](private_equity_guide.md), and it explains the absence of fund names, ISINs, prospectuses and performance tables (§5.1).
- **Real estate is the anchor and the physical footprint.** Direct, asset-level ownership in gateway cities, held through named SPVs (SV Robinson, SVRE Australia, SVRE UK and smaller SV entities) — with the group's own head office at 71 Robinson Road, the asset it bought in 2019 ✅. This is the business the firm's chairman founded the group around, and it is the one with a fully public, dated deal record (§6.2).
- **Fund of funds is the diversifier and the external-manager channel.** The firm "view[s] our fund managers as trusted partners" and constructs "robust and diversified portfolio[s] … across a broad spectrum of asset classes" ✅. This is the silo that generates the firm's *indirect* exposure — the externality a bank sees as a stream of capital calls and distributions rather than as owned assets (§11.5).
- **Private equity is split between funds and direct.** The firm commits to private-equity funds (led by the Head of Private Markets Fund Investments) *and* makes direct investments (led by a dedicated Head of Private Equity Direct Investment), with "selected portfolio companies" published on the site ✅. This dual structure — fund commitments plus control-or-influence direct stakes — is the most operationally demanding of the three silos for a bank (§11.5).
- **Public markets and fixed income are the liquidity and cash-management layer.** The LinkedIn and directory descriptions name "public equities, fixed income" alongside real estate, private equity and alternatives ✅, and no public strategy detail exists beyond that listing ⚠.
- **The Isle of Man corporate office is the group's back-office layer.** The CFO "oversees the Corporate Office in the Isle of Man", and the group's Isle of Man companies sit behind a **fund-services administrator** (Suntera) — a structure consistent with holding and administration functions for the group's vehicles rather than with a retail-facing fund platform ✅/⚠ (§7.2).

Read together, the model is **capital preservation and compounding across asset classes, run from a Singapore balance sheet, with real estate as the visible expression of the firm's conviction and value.** A bank should hold two things at once: the *asset logic* is clear and verifiable (named buildings, dated deals, a professional team), while the *capital logic* — how much is equity versus debt, who the ultimate beneficial owners are, and what the firm's liabilities look like — is entirely undisclosed ⚠ and must be requested, not assumed.

The distinguishing conventions this guide does not re-derive: carried-interest and GP economics, capital-call/distribution waterfalls, and LP/GP alignment clauses (cross-ref the [Private Equity Guide](private_equity_guide.md)); the Singapore fund-vehicle wrappers (VCC) and the 13O/13U incentive regime (cross-ref [Hedge Funds Singapore](hedge_funds_singapore_guide.md) and [Singapore Private Markets](singapore_private_markets_guide.md)); and the alternatives product taxonomy (cross-ref [Asset Management Alternatives](asset_management_alternatives_guide.md)).

## 7. The Licensing and the Compliance

### 7.1 The MAS Question — a Verified Negative

Query: the **MAS Financial Institutions Directory** (eservices.mas.gov.sg/fid — "Find a financial institution, licence type or activity in Singapore") was searched for **"sunventure"** (single token) and for **"sun venture"** (two tokens). Result: **"No result for: sunventure"**, with the directory's own suggestion reading "Did you mean: **bioventure**" ✅ verified negative (queried September 2026; the directory carries a standing anti-scam warning banner directing users to verify an institution's identity against it).

The single result returned for the "sunventure" token was **TRINITY INNOVATION BIOVENTURE SINGAPORE PTE. LTD.** (a Capital Markets Services licensee at 2 Science Park Drive) — an unrelated entity that merely contains the substring "venture". The looser two-token query returned 387 sector-wide results (banks, CMS licensees, insurers, payment institutions and the like) **none of which was any Sun Venture entity** ✅.

Consequence for the Singapore entities: **no Sun Venture entity appears in the MAS directory in any capacity** — not as a bank, not as a **CMS-licensed** (capital markets services) fund manager or dealer, not as a **licensed financial adviser**, not as an **RFMC or LFMC**-tier fund manager, not as a licensed payment institution or money-changer under the Payment Services Act, and not as an insurer or broker. In plain terms: **there is no MAS licence of any kind behind the Sun Venture name in Singapore as at the query date** — see §7.3 for what that does and does not mean. (Regime cross-ref, condensed: the Singapore licensing architecture — the SFA CMS regime, the RFMC/LFMC fund-management tiers, the VCC wrapper and the 13O/13U incentive regime — is the subject of the [MAS Regulations, Guidelines and Industry Expectations](mas_regulations_guidelines_guide.md) and [Hedge Funds Singapore](hedge_funds_singapore_guide.md) guides and is not re-derived here.)

This is a **particularly interesting negative** in this case, because one of the group's own Singapore entities — **SUN VENTURE PTE. LTD.** (UEN 201114838M) — carries the SSIC code **66309, "Fund management activities n.e.c."**, with a secondary code of **68209, "Real estate activities on a fee or commission basis"** ✅. A bank's instinct on reading that code is to expect a CMS licence. The register says there is none. The reconciliation is the classic one for principal investors and family offices: **an entity can be *coded* as conducting fund-management activities without being *licensed* to manage third-party money**, because the SSIC code describes an activity classification for statistical and administrative purposes while the CMS licensing requirement attaches to specific regulated activities conducted for third parties (and exempt regimes exist for entities managing only their own or related-party assets) ⚠ (regime analysis; see §7.3 — this is *not* a legal opinion that the firm is exempt).

## 10. The Financial / Investment Analysis

### 10.1 Singapore Property-Investment Economics

To read the group's Singapore transactions as an investment analyst — rather than as a registries analyst — requires four economic primitives, all of them general market mechanics (industry practice; not firm disclosures) ⚠:

- **Capitalisation rate and the income-capitalisation method.** A commercial asset's value is commonly derived as net property income divided by a capitalisation rate: value = NPI ÷ cap rate. A 4.5% cap rate implies a price of roughly 22× net income; a 5.5% cap rate implies roughly 18×. Singapore CBD prime office cap rates compressed through the 2010s and the early 2020s, which is exactly why the *same* asset can be "expensive" on a per-square-foot basis and "cheap" on a yield basis at different points in the cycle ⚠ (market mechanic).
- **Yield compression as the return driver.** Where cap rates fall, asset values rise even with flat rents — meaning that a buyer who entered early and exited after compression earns a substantial part of its return from *re-rating* rather than from operations. The Westgate Tower sequence is the clearest available illustration: acquired 2014 at **S$579m**, sold 2022 at **S$680m** (≈S$2,230 psf) — a ≈17% uplift over eight years ✅, which on the published figures is modest and consistent with an income-plus-modest-compression outcome rather than a speculative re-rate ⚠ (arithmetic on published prices, not a return calculation).
- **The 99-year leasehold clock.** Most Singapore commercial land is leasehold; the residual lease term shortens the income period and depresses value relative to freehold. This matters directly to the assets in the record: 120 Moorgate in London is **freehold** ✅, and the Hyatt Place hotel is **freehold** ✅, whereas 71 Robinson Road sits on a **leasehold site with "74 years remaining"** as reported at the 2019 sale ✅ (Mingtiandi) — a decaying asset-life term that any financing analysis must amortise against the loan tenor.
- **Debt and the SPV/MPC structure.** Singapore real-estate deals are typically held through special-purpose vehicles and often financed at the SPV level with a mortgage, with the sponsor's equity behind it. The group's own structure is consistent with this — it bought **equity interests** in an SPV (CR-71 Robinson Road Singapore Pte. Ltd.) rather than the land directly ✅, and it houses assets in SV/SVRE entities ✅ — which means the bank's credit exposure, if it lends, sits against a specific asset's cash flow and a specific SPV's covenants, not against a consolidated group balance sheet the bank cannot see ⚠ (structure inference from public registry and counsel disclosures).

### 10.3 SPV, PCC and Financing Structures

The group's public entity architecture tells a coherent story about how it finances and holds assets, and that story is the most useful thing a bank can take from this section:

- **Financing is not public.** No lender, facility, LTV, interest cover, hedging arrangement or security package is disclosed anywhere ❌. What the bank can infer is the *shape* of the financing need — per-asset debt against SPVs, in SGD, GBP and AUD, with leasehold (71 Robinson) and freehold (Moorgate, Hyatt Place) collateral of different legal quality, plus committed capital calls into private-equity and fund-of-funds positions ⚠ — and the shape is enough to design the relationship, not to price the risk.

Peer *facts* are only useful if verified, and the guide keeps the comparison narrow and sourced. The transaction record itself supplies the most reliable comparables, because they are the deals against which the firm actually transacted:

- **The seller-side comparables on 71 Robinson Road.** Commerz Real had bought the then-unfinished asset from Lehman Brothers and Kajima Overseas Asia (developers at a cost of ~S$613m) in 2008 at a then-record **S$3,125 psf**, and sold to Sun Venture in 2019 at **S$2,756 psf** — a **~12% mark-down** eleven years later ✅ (Mingtiandi). - **The Sydney comparable.** 50 Miller Street sits two minutes' walk from **73 Miller Street**, a 13-storey office block that ESR and Partners Capital acquired from UBS in an **A$400m** deal closing the month before ✅ (Mingtiandi) — a direct, dated comparable for the North Sydney submarket.

And **the comparables are transaction prices, not valuations**: none of the figures above is a published independent valuation of a Sun Venture asset, and the only published third-party valuation in the record relates to a different asset entirely (Savills' **S$688.0m** valuation of 21 Collyer Quay for CICT as at 31 October 2024, a building that sold to a **Haidilao-linked family** rather than to Sun Venture ✅ — recorded here precisely to dispose of the widely repeated but incorrect attribution of that deal to this group ❌).

## 11. The Cymbal Bank Worked Example — A Multi-Asset Investment Group as a Corporate Client

### 11.1 The Scenario

**Cymbal Bank** — the repository's fictional Singapore full-bank persona (conventions per the [MAS Regulations, Guidelines and Industry Expectations](mas_regulations_guidelines_guide.md) guide and the worked examples of the [Hedge Funds Singapore](hedge_funds_singapore_guide.md) and [Private Equity Singapore](private_equity_singapore_guide.md) guides) — onboards the Singapore holding company of a **Sun-Venture-style multi-asset investment group** as a corporate client. The model client follows the subject's verified shape: **SUN VENTURE REAL ESTATE (SINGAPORE) PTE. LTD.** (UEN 202226362M, Live, SSIC 64202, registered office 71 Robinson Road #04-01 ✅) as the account-holding group entity; **SUN VENTURE PTE. LTD.** (201114838M, SSIC 66309, former name LEGEND CORPORATE MANAGEMENT ✅) as the group's fund-management-and-services company; **SUN VENTURE DEVELOPMENTS PTE. LTD.** (200804868M, SSIC 68101 ✅) as the property development arm; and **SV ROBINSON PTE. LTD.** (201918338N ✅) as the flagship asset SPV. The group approaches Cymbal Bank for: SGD, USD, GBP and AUD operating accounts; segregated SPV and escrow accounts; an asset-level mortgage facility for a Singapore office tower; subscription and distribution processing for its third-party fund commitments; custody of cash and listed collateral; cross-border payments to the UK, Australia and the Isle of Man; and FX. ⚠ All product parameters below are illustrative fictional constructs for the worked example, not representations about the real firm's banking arrangements.

### 11.2 Onboarding and KYB — the Name-Screening Disambiguation in Practice

This is the worked example's distinctive moment, and it follows directly from §2. The KYB file for "Sun Venture" will **collide with the name cluster before it meets the real client**, and — unusually — the *hardest* hits to dispose of are the client's own former entities:

- **Screening hits to expect and reject (with documented reasons).** (i) **SUN VENTURE CAPITAL PTE. LTD.** (200708199C, Struck Off, 80 Raffles Place) — same name stem, different company, struck off, no address or UEN overlap; (ii) **SUN VENTURES PTE. LTD.** (202441052W, Live, general trading, at a 57-company Orchard Road service address) — different legal name (plural), different activity, no overlap; (iii) **SUN VENTURES (52952508M, Terminated)** and (iv) the **Florida LLCs** (SUN VENTURES GROUP LLC L24000089507; SUN VENTURE LLC) — unrelated US state filings; (v) **"Sun Ventures" VC** page — unrelated; and (vi) the **Altss "Venture Capital" category page** — an aggregator miscategorisation of the client itself. Each hit must be dispositioned in the CDD file with the reason it is *not* the client.
- **The most dangerous hits — the client's own dead entities.** **SUN VENTURE LAND (201005538D), SUN VENTURE GROUP (201131020D), SUN VENTURE HOMES (201304448R), SUN VENTURE REALTY (201304452D), SUN VENTURE INVESCO (201000948M), SUN VENTURE PROPERTY (201024383D), SUN VENTURE COMMERCIAL (201109478H)** and **SUN VENTURE (S) INVESTMENTS (200807082E)** are **all Struck Off** and **all at or near 50 Scotts Road** — the address the client's live entities used until the 2025 move to 71 Robinson Road ✅. - **The registry extracts the CDD file must hold.** The ACRA business profile for UEN **202226362M** (Live, SSIC 64202, registered office 71 Robinson Road #04-01, auditor Foo Kon Tan LLP, annual return 30 July 2025); the profiles for **201114838M** and **200804868M** (including the **former-name history** LEGEND CORPORATE MANAGEMENT / SV MANAGEMENT SERVICES / DB & B DEVELOPMENTS, which is the group's identity chain made documentary); the profile for the asset SPV **201918338N**; the **Isle of Man** company records for 017723V and 017724V ⚠ (aggregator/LEI-sourced) showing the dissolved PCC and the asset-management company; and the **verified MAS negative** — the Financial Institutions Directory "No result for: sunventure" finding (§7.1) — filed as a dated register screenshot so the file *proves* the client holds no MAS licence rather than assuming it.
- **UBO and control.** The natural persons to identify and verify are **Ricky Au (Chairman and founder)** and the named leadership (HC Yang, YP Chiang, Ying Jian Chan), plus the directors and shareholders shown on the ACRA extracts and the Isle of Man records; the group's own description of being "backed by **Taiwanese and Singaporean investors**" ✅ (Eversheds, 2019) is a *request-for-information* item, not an answer, and the Isle of Man layer means UBO tracing may run through the administrator's records ⚠.
- **Expected-activity mapping.** SSIC 64202 (holding companies) for the group entity must be reconciled with the *actual* declared activity — investment holding, SPV funding, capital calls into external funds, distributions back up, and payments to UK/Australia/IoM entities — so the account's activity profile is consistent from day one; and SSIC **66309 (fund management)** on the services company must be explicitly documented as **not** indicating a CMS licence (§7.1).

**The CDD file at a glance** — the artefact list a Cymbal Bank relationship file should hold for this client, following the conventions of the [MAS Regulations, Guidelines and Industry Expectations](mas_regulations_guidelines_guide.md) and [Investment Portfolio Operations](investment_portfolio_operations_guide.md) overlays:

- The ACRA business profiles for 202226362M, 201114838M, 200804868M and 201918338N (printed, dated) — entity names, statuses, SSIC codes, former names, registered offices, auditors.
- The Isle of Man company records for 017723V and 017724V, with a note that the latter dissolved in 2023 and that the **IoM FSA register remains an open check** ⚠.

- The name-cluster disposition log — every lookalike hit (the struck-off namesake, the plural-form trading company, the US LLCs, the VC page) **and every one of the client's own struck-off legacy entities**, each with the reason it is not (or is a retired part of) the client.
- A **group entity chart** (Singapore holdco → SPVs → IoM layer → UK/Australia SPVs) with the evidence for each arrow marked ✅/⚠, so intercompany flows are identifiable at a glance.
- UBO/control documentation for the named principals, with source-of-wealth taken at the level the risk rating requires, and a documented note on any UBO concentration.
- The expected-activity memo mapping SSIC codes to the declared flows (capital in, SPV deployment, distributions, offshore administration fees), signed by the relationship manager.

The onboarding conclusion a well-run file reaches: **the client is an unregulated-in-Singapore multi-asset investment group** (verified MAS negative on file), **not** a licensed fund manager, a VASP or a sanctioned entity — and every lookalike that says otherwise has been dispositioned in writing, including the client's own corporate history.

### 11.3 Real-Estate Financing and Lending

For a group whose capital structure is opaque but whose **assets are named and dated** ✅, the right lending posture is **asset-backed and SPV-level**, not balance-sheet-based:

| Facility | Borrower | Security and structure (illustrative ⚠) | The bank's questions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Investment-property mortgage** | **SV ROBINSON PTE. LTD.** (201918338N) — the SPV that owns 71 Robinson Road | First legal mortgage over the property; assignment of leases and rental income; charge over the SPV's shares as additional security; covenants at SPV level | Residual lease term (**74 years at 2019** ✅) versus loan tenor; tenant concentration (WeWork, Commerzbank, Visa, Ogilvy ✅); revaluation cycle; hedging of the rate |
| **Development or refurbishment facility** | **SUN VENTURE DEVELOPMENTS PTE. LTD.** (200804868M) — the SSIC 68101 arm | Construction-stage drawdown against a cost-to-complete and a QS certificate; interest reserve; completion guarantee from the sponsor | Track record (the 2008 URA tender project, 50 Scotts Road ✅) and the sponsor's equity contribution; planning and TOP milestones |

| **Subscription / capital-call facility** | The group holdco | A committed line bridging capital calls into private-equity and fund-of-funds commitments, secured against the portfolio of fund interests | Whether the firm's commitments are documented and assignable — and the bank's own policy on lending against fund interests (cross-ref [Private Equity](private_equity_guide.md)) |

The design principle for a Sun-Venture-style client is stated plainly: **the bank underwrites the building it can see and the SPV it can charge, and does not pretend to underwrite a group balance sheet it cannot see.** Where the bank wants group-level comfort, it asks for it — audited group accounts, a capital-and-commitments schedule, and an asset-by-asset book — and treats the client's willingness to provide them as itself a risk signal ⚠ (recommendation).

### 11.4 Treasury, FX and Custody

The group's currency and cash profile follows directly from its asset map (§6.2), and it is the reason a multi-asset client needs a full treasury conversation rather than a loan and a cheque book:

| Need | The group's shape (from public facts) | Cymbal Bank's product response (illustrative ⚠) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Multi-currency operating accounts** | SGD (Singapore HQ, holdco and SPVs), GBP (four London assets), AUD (Sydney), USD (fund commitments and cross-border pricing), plus IOM administration fees | A multi-currency pooling structure per entity, with SPV sub-ledgers so project cash does not co-mingle with group cash |
| **Escrow and segregated accounts** | Asset acquisitions and any third-party fund commitments require escrow timing (the 2019 deal was structured as a **share purchase of an SPV** ✅, which means consideration held pending transfer conditions) | Escrow accounts per transaction; subscription/commitment accounts where capital is called |
| **FX and hedging** | SGD/GBP/AUD/USD conversion for distributions, rent sweeps, debt service and administration fees; GBP and AUD asset income repatriated to a SGD centre | Spot and forward FX; interest-rate hedging where an SPV carries floating-rate debt; payments to IoM administrator accounts |
| **Custody** | The bankable assets are **cash, listed securities and any hedging collateral** — *not* the buildings | Cash and securities custody; no attempt to custody real estate (as the [Investment Portfolio Operations](investment_portfolio_operations_guide.md) guide's scope discipline requires) |
| **Offshore payments** | Recurring administration fees to **Suntera Fund Services (IOM) Limited** ✅; possible intra-group funding to the IoM layer | Mapped as intra-group flows against the entity chart, with the IoM purpose documented |

The custody scoping point is the one a real-estate group forces a bank to get right: **the buildings are not at the bank.** The bank holds the group's cash, its collateral and its payment rails; the assets that make the firm's public record live in land registries and SPV share registers the bank will never custody. The bank's product is the *money layer* around the assets — which is why the flow design in §11.5 matters more than asset custody.

### 11.5 Private-Markets Operational Flows and Fund Administration

Here the group's **fund of funds and private-equity commitments** create a flow pattern distinct from its property income — a pattern a bank must recognise to avoid both missed risk and false alerts:

- **Capital in (commitments called):** a third-party fund's administrator issues a capital call; the group's holdco funds the commitment from its subscription/escrow account; each inbound-to-the-fund wire is a **source-of-funds review point** (which entity is paying, into which fund, against which commitment schedule). For the group's own internal funding, the flows are *intra-group* and must map to the entity chart (§11.2).
- **Distributions out and up:** fund distributions arrive back to the group (return of capital, income, gains); where the group distributes to its shareholder, the outbound run is periodic and single-beneficiary. **This is the classic red-flag *shape*** — periodic, similar-amount outbound wires to the same beneficiary — and the bank's monitoring must clear it against the *documented* ownership and mandate picture on file, which for a family office is a shareholder distribution rather than an investor waterfall ⚠ (pattern-recognition point; cross-ref the distribution mechanics of the [Private Equity Guide](private_equity_guide.md) and [Private Equity Singapore](private_equity_singapore_guide.md) guides).
- **Fund administration.** The group's **offshore entities are administered by a professional fund administrator (Suntera)** ✅ — so the bank's counterparties for confirmations, statements and KYC refresh on the IoM side are that administrator, not the client's staff; and the dissolved PCC (2023) ✅/⚠ should be recorded as a *closed* vehicle with a documented reason rather than left as an unexplained name in the entity chart.
- **Sub-lines and recallable capital.** Fund interests bring subscription lines, recallable distributions and equalisation mechanics; a bank providing a subscription-line-like facility must know which commitments are still callable — a question only the client can answer, and one the CDD file should record as an open item ⚠.

**A worked distribution flow** (illustrative arithmetic in the style of the repository's worked examples — fictional parameters, not a representation about the real firm ⚠): suppose the group's Singapore holdco receives SGD 9.0m of rent from the 71 Robinson SPV, GBP 2.4m of London distributions converted to SGD, and a USD 1.2m fund distribution converted to SGD, and pays a shareholder distribution of SGD 10.0m. At Cymbal Bank the run looks like: three inbound receipts (SGD, GBP, USD) → FX conversion at published spot with a documented spread → consolidation in the holdco's SGD operating account → one SGD 10.0m outbound wire to the shareholder. The bank's monitoring flags the *shape* (periodic, single-beneficiary) and clears it against the shareholder documentation on file; the FX confirmations and account statements supply the group's own record-keeping. The arithmetic is deliberately unremarkable: for a bank, a well-documented periodic rhythm is **less** suspicious than an erratic one, which is why the expected-activity memo (§11.2) matters more than any single alert threshold.

### 11.6 The Bank's Reporting and Compliance Overlay

- **MAS-facing:** Cymbal Bank applies its full bank overlay to the accounts — **MAS Notice 626/626A** AML/CFT obligations, transaction monitoring, sanctions and adverse-news screening across the group's flows *and* its counterparties (SPVs, the offshore administrator, the fund managers and the portfolio companies — including the crypto-adjacent one) and **CRS/FATCA** classification of the group's cross-border relationships ✅ (obligations map cross-ref the [MAS Regulations, Guidelines and Industry Expectations](mas_regulations_guidelines_guide.md) guide).
- **Periodic review:** because the client is *unlicensed in Singapore*, the bank's periodic review cycle is the client's only regulatory heartbeat — annual refresh of the registry extracts (UENs still Live? filings still current? auditor still disclosed? any entity struck off? any *new* entity added, especially offshore?), re-screen of the name cluster (any new "Sun Venture" entrant, and has any legacy entity been restored?), and re-test of the expected-activity profile against actual flows. The 2025 registered-office change (50 Scotts Road → 71 Robinson Road) is exactly the sort of drift periodic review should catch and document ⚠.

The periodic-review checklist, in calendar form (fictional parameters following the [MAS Regulations, Guidelines and Industry Expectations](mas_regulations_guidelines_guide.md) guide's Cymbal Bank overlay):

| Check | Owner | Frequency | What would trigger action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **Offshore-layer refresh: IoM companies, administrator, any new offshore entity** | Cymbal Bank KYC | Annual | A new Isle of Man or other offshore vehicle → re-onboarding trigger |

| Client's own disclosures (new assets, new vehicles, new asset classes) | Relationship manager | Event-driven | Any change to the §6 asset map or the §2.2 entity chain |

- **Investor-relations and reporting support:** a family-office principal reports to its shareholder, not to a public LP base — but if the group ever raises external capital, the bank's statements, FX confirmations and custody marks become the input to an *investor*-facing report. The bank feeds the numbers; it does not become the administrator, mirroring the discipline of the [Investment Portfolio Operations](investment_portfolio_operations_guide.md) and [Private Equity Singapore](private_equity_singapore_guide.md) guides.
- **The boundary to keep documented:** the bank is *not* the group's compliance officer and *not* its regulator. The open perimeter questions of §7.3 — the Isle of Man position, the former PCC, and whether any group entity manages third-party money anywhere — belong in the bank's risk-appetite conversation as **material-change triggers**, not as standing assumptions. If the group launches a pooled vehicle, appears on a regulator register, adds a materially different asset class, or adds a new offshore entity, that is a **re-onboarding event**, not a note.

### 11.7 The Lessons

Six lessons generalise from this worked example to any bank meeting a Sun-Venture-style client. **First, identity is the product:** for a name as crowded as "Sun Venture" — where the client's *own* struck-off entities pollute the screening universe — the CDD file's disambiguation evidence (UENs, former names, address history, register negatives, dispositioned lookalikes) *is* the KYB deliverable. **Second, the former-name trail is the identity chain:** LEGEND CORPORATE MANAGEMENT, LEGEND ASSET MANAGEMENT and LEGEND GLOBAL OPPORTUNITIES, read together with the chairman's own account and the 2019 counsel note, tell the group's story better than any brand asset — and a bank that ignores former names will fail to connect the entities at all. **Third, unlicensed is not simple:** the verified absence of a MAS licence, sitting next to an SSIC *fund-management* code, makes the client *harder* to monitor, not easier — the bank's periodic review substitutes for a regulator's, and the perimeter question must be documented as open. **Fourth, underwrite the asset, not the aura:** the firm's capital structure is opaque and its asset record is public, so credit should be SPV-level against named, dated, verifiable collateral. **Fifth, map the money to the model:** rent in, distributions out, fund calls and fund distributions, offshore administration fees — a bank that understands the multi-asset permanent-capital model can distinguish legitimate periodic flows from red-flag-shaped ones. **Sixth, watch the offshore layer:** the Isle of Man corporate office and the dissolved PCC are the least-understood part of this client — the bank should check the IoM FSA register rather than inherit the assumption that an offshore administrator's address means an unregulated shell, and equally should not assume the opposite.

## 16. The Closing Summary

Sun Venture is a study in how much a disciplined researcher can verify about a private investment firm **when the assets are public and the balance sheet is not**. The verified core is solid and unusually rich for a firm that talks to the media only through its advisers: a Singapore-headquartered multi-asset investment firm that says it was **founded in 2006** out of Chairman **Ricky Au's Legend Investment Holdings** in Hong Kong; a documented Singapore entity chain running from **SUN VENTURE DEVELOPMENTS PTE. LTD. (UEN 200804868M, 2008)** through **SUN VENTURE PTE. LTD. (UEN 201114838M, 2011, formerly LEGEND CORPORATE MANAGEMENT)** to **SUN VENTURE REAL ESTATE (SINGAPORE) PTE. LTD. (UEN 202226362M, 2022)**, all registered at **71 Robinson Road #04-01** — the building the group bought in 2019; an Isle of Man corporate arm whose own former names carry the **LEGEND** stem and whose protected-cell fund company **dissolved in April 2023**; a leadership roster of named professionals with verifiable prior employers (Temasek, GIC, Income Insurance, Allgreen, BreadTalk, Yeo Hiap Seng); and a cross-border property record of transactions with prices, dates, yields, sellers and advisers attached — **71 Robinson Road at S$655 million (2019)**, **1 New Oxford Street at £174 million (2020)**, **1 & 2 New Ludgate at £552 million (2020)**, **120 Moorgate at £148 million on a 4.5% net initial yield (2022)**, **50 Miller Street, North Sydney at A$148 million (2022)** and **Hyatt Place London City East at under £100 million (2024)** — plus the disposal of **Westgate Tower to AEW for S$680 million (2022)**. What is equally clear, and equally important, is what the record refuses to say: **no AUM figure exists anywhere**, no fund names, no fee or leverage terms, no financial statements, no investor list, and no legal analysis of the group's regulatory perimeter — and the MAS Financial Institutions Directory, queried directly, returns **"No result for: sunventure"**, a verified negative sitting uncomfortably beside an in-group company whose SSIC code literally reads *fund management activities*, and beside an Isle of Man asset-management company whose regulator's register has yet to be checked. Everything unverified in this guide is flagged ⚠ or ❌ rather than smoothed, per the repository's honesty convention.

For a bank, Sun Venture is not an exotic client — it is a *representative* one, and a more instructive one than most: a professionally staffed, family-office-rooted, multi-asset principal investor whose properties are named and dated, whose capital structure is invisible, whose name collides with unrelated firms **and with its own struck-off corporate ancestors**, and whose money moves in the recognisable rhythms of rent sweeps, capital calls, fund distributions and offshore administration fees. The worked example showed what that means in practice: a CDD file that proves *which* Sun Venture by UEN, former name and address history rather than by brand; a lending posture that underwrites the building and the SPV instead of an unseeable group balance sheet; a treasury and custody design built around four currencies and assets the bank will never hold; a monitoring overlay that reads periodic shareholder distributions as documented mechanics while still testing them; a periodic review that checks the offshore layer as carefully as the onshore one because the client has no regulator to do it for them; and a re-onboarding trigger ready for the day the group adds an entity, a vehicle or a register entry. In an industry where private capital earns its welcome through disclosure it chooses to give rather than disclosure it is compelled to give, the bank that documents before it trusts, flags what it cannot prove, and reads the register — onshore and offshore alike — for the moment the story changes is the bank that earns the venture's record.
