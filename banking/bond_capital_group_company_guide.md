### 1.3 The Entity Type — Exempt Private Company Limited by Shares (Verified)

The company type is **Exempt Private Company Limited by Shares** — **Verified** against companies.sg ("a Singapore Exempt Private Company Limited by Shares"), sgpbusiness.com ("a Exempt Private Company Limited by Shares"), opengovsg.com ("Exempt Private Company Limited by Shares", entity type "Local Company"), recordowl.com, companieshouse.sg and sgpdirectory.com.

What the type means, in the corporate-structure vocabulary of [../management/mba_body_of_knowledge_guide.md](../management/mba_body_of_knowledge_guide.md):

- **Private company limited by shares** — a company whose members' liability is limited to their shareholding, whose shares are not offered to the public, and whose membership is capped (50 members for an exempt private company). The standard vehicle for a founder- or family-controlled holding/investment structure.
- **Exempt** — the Companies Act's "exempt private company" designation applies to a private company with **no more than 20 members** where **no member is a corporation** (with exceptions). The practical consequences are the exempt-company perks: audit exemptions for small companies, simplified reporting, and — critically for the thin-public-footprint story — **no public filing of financial statements**. An exempt private company's accounts are private: this is *why* there are no annual reports to analyse, and why this guide's honest framing leans on the registry record, the website, and licence registers rather than financial statements.
- **Local company** — incorporated under the Companies Act in Singapore, as opposed to a foreign-company branch or a variable capital company (§6.3).

The takeaway: the entity type is the *standard* choice for a founder/family-controlled investment-holding structure — it maximises privacy and minimises compliance burden, exactly what the thin public footprint reflects. It is also the same type used by the same-suite Bond Capital Partners, Bond Capital Group, and Bond Financial Group — a consistent family pattern worth noting (§3.3).

### 1.8 The Thin-Public-Footprint Note — a Method Disclaimer

It is worth being explicit about what the absence of information means in this guide. Bond Capital Group is a **privately held exempt private company** — five years old at the time of writing — and its public footprint is thin **by construction**, not by accident:

1. **No public financials.** As an exempt private company it is not required to (and does not) file its accounts publicly. There is no balance sheet, no revenue line, no AUM figure, no portfolio valuation anywhere in the public record — and this guide will not invent one. The S$1M paid-up capital (§1.5) is the *only* capital figure in the public record.
2. **No press coverage.** Targeted searches for the entity name, the UEN, and the "Bond Capital" brand returned essentially no independent press — no Business Times, Straits Times, or industry-trade coverage. RecordOwl's own media monitor confirms: "No recent media coverage found… This company has not been mentioned in the news sources we monitor." This is normal for a private investment-holding firm and the guide's framing reflects it.
3. **The narrative sources are directories and a website that footers to another entity.** The "BCP mandate" exists as directory blurbs (RecordOwl on both the Group and Partners pages; Infobel, Datanyze, CB Insights for the Partners brand) and as the bond.sg website, whose footer reads **"COPYRIGHT 2022 BOND CAPITAL PARTNERS PTE LTD"** — i.e., the only website in the brand cluster legally belongs to the *other* same-address entity. Everything beyond the registry record is therefore quoted and flagged, never asserted.
4. **What IS verifiable is verified.** The registry record (§1), the website text (§2), the same-address facts (§3), the absence of a MAS record (§5), and the market context (§6) are all checkable, and the claims-status ledger in §10 tracks each.

The honest consequence: this guide can tell you exactly what the public record says, exactly what the brand publishes, and exactly where the two diverge — but it cannot tell you the firm's size, portfolio, returns, or the legal ownership map of the #33-00 cluster. That is the nature of the subject, and the guide wears it openly.

### 5.2 What an Investment-Holding Firm Does and Does Not Need (Verified Context)

For the corporate-structure vocabulary (cross-ref [../management/mba_body_of_knowledge_guide.md](../management/mba_body_of_knowledge_guide.md)): a **holding company** owns shares in operating/portfolio companies and provides group-level functions (treasury, brand, management, consultancy); an **investment-holding company** (SSIC 64202) is the same form with an investment purpose — holding shares, real estate, or other assets for the owners' account. Singapore's treatment:

- **Incorporation and maintenance:** ACRA registration, annual return, and (for a small exempt private company) minimal audit obligations — all satisfied on the record (§1.6).
- **MAS licensing:** none required for holding/consultancy activity itself. Licensing attaches to *activities* (fund management under the Securities and Futures Act; payment services under the Payment Services Act; financial advice under the FAA), not to the corporate form.
- **The exemption nuance:** if the holding company's business grows into *managing third-party capital* — a fund with outside investors — the fund-management licensing machinery (RFMC → LFMC/CMS route, or 13O-compatible exempt structures) activates; that is the §5.3 boundary and the §7 worked example's design question.
- **What a holding firm *does* need in practice:** substance expectations from its bankers (the "thin-footprint" red flags a bank's onboarding team will probe — see §1.8), tax filings (IRAS), and — if it ever holds MAS-regulated subsidiaries — the group-level governance that comes with them. The S$1M paid-up capital (§1.5) is a meaningful substance signal to onboarding teams: it is real money committed to the vehicle, well above shell minimums.

### 5.3 The Fund-Management Question — SFA, RFMC, 13O (Verified Context)

The one licensing question this firm's *published mandate* makes live: **does "manages capital globally on behalf of its shareholders" cross into fund management?** The answer, per the MAS framework (cross-ref [singapore_private_markets_guide.md](singapore_private_markets_guide.md) §5.7):

- **No — as published.** The SFA's fund-management definition (s.2(1): "managing a portfolio of capital markets products or other assets" on behalf of a client/scheme) is engaged when managing *third-party* money — a collective investment scheme or client portfolios. Managing the shareholders' *own* capital through a holding-company vehicle is proprietary investing, not regulated fund management. This is precisely why the BCP-style design (investment-holding wrapper + proprietary capital + manager-selection sleeve + direct private co-investment) can run without a capital-markets services (CMS) licence — the design's elegance is that the *licensable activity* never starts.
- **Yes — if the model changes.** The moment third-party capital arrives (a fund with outside investors, managed accounts for others, a 13O fund vehicle with investors beyond the family), the operator needs either: (a) **registered fund manager (RFMC)** status; (b) a **licensed fund manager (LFMC) / full CMS licence**; or (c) a structure engineered around an **exempt** route (e.g., the single-family-office exemption for a 13D/13O family vehicle — see [singapore_private_markets_guide.md](singapore_private_markets_guide.md) §6.4). No evidence of any of these exists for the cluster ⚠ — consistent with the proprietary-capital reading.
- **The 13O overlay:** a 13O fund tax-incentive application (SG-incorporated vehicle + SG-based manager) is the standard companion when a proprietary/holding structure wants the *tax* wrapper for its investments — but 13O is a tax incentive administered by MAS/IRAS, not a licence, and it is *conditioned* on the manager being SG-based and the vehicle being a qualifying fund (typically a VCC) — §6.3 and the §7 design.

### 6.3 The VCC Regime and the 13O/13U/13D Schemes (Verified Context)

The regime that defines modern SG wealth structuring — cross-ref [singapore_private_markets_guide.md](singapore_private_markets_guide.md) §5 and §6.4 for the full treatment:

- **VCC** — the Variable Capital Company (Variable Capital Companies Act, in force 14 January 2020): a corporate fund vehicle with variable capital, segregated umbrella sub-funds, and redomiciliation optionality. The VCC is Singapore's answer to the Cayman/BVI fund vehicle — onshore, MAS/ACRA-administered, and now the default choice for SG-domiciled funds and family investment vehicles.
- **Section 13O** — the onshore fund tax incentive: a Singapore-incorporated vehicle (e.g., a VCC) managed by a Singapore-based manager qualifies for tax exemption on specified income; the scheme (with 13U and 13D) is extended to **31 December 2029**.
- **Section 13U** — the enhanced-tier scheme for offshore vehicles/managers (S$50 million minimum, local-substance conditions — reported).
- **Section 13D** — the dedicated single-family-office scheme: a fund vehicle owned and controlled by the family, managed by a Singapore-based manager, no third-party investors — the default wrapper for the SFO population.
- **13OA** — extends 13O-style treatment to Singapore LPs for small PE/VC funds (2024 amendments).

The reason the regime matters to *this* guide: the worked example in §7 builds a BCP-style investment-holding architecture on exactly this machinery — the VCC umbrella as the investment vehicle, 13O/13D as the tax wrapper, and the holding companies as the legal centre. **Bond Capital Group itself makes no fund claim whatsoever** — the VCC/13O machinery is context, not evidence about the firm ⚠ — but it is the single most important thing to understand about the market the firm chose to incorporate into, and the most likely *destination* if the cluster's mandate ever formalises into a fund.

- **Section 13O** as the tax wrapper (SG-incorporated vehicle + SG-based manager — the cleanest substance story), with **13D** as the documented alternative if the vehicle stays strictly single-family, and **13U** noted as the fallback if an offshore vehicle is ever required.
- A **registered fund manager (RFMC)** — or the exempt single-family route where applicable — running the VCC's day-to-day, sized to the family's actual mandate rather than assumed (the licensing choice from the private-markets guide's §5.7).

**Step 4 — the governance and disclosure layer (the anti-thin-footprint plan).** The design's most important element is *deliberate substance* — the exact opposite of the #33-00 cluster's pattern: audited (or at least independently reviewed) group accounts; a **public-facing entity directory** on the family office's website naming each entity with its UEN, SSIC codes and status; a documented **licensing map** (MAS status for each entity, manager registrations, and a statement of the proprietary-capital basis on which no CMS licence is held); and a **name-collision mitigation pack** (the §3.3 lesson): UENs displayed on invoices and contracts, a domain portfolio, and boilerplate distinguishing the family's entities from the "Bond"-named firms and every other similarly named group. The family also resolves the *directory-conflation* hazard the real cluster suffers: one website, one brand name, one entity directory — no ambiguity about which legal entity "manages capital."

### 7.5 The Lessons

1. **The BCP blueprint is coherent — and its licensing magic is the proprietary-capital boundary.** The published mandate ("manages capital on behalf of its shareholders", §2.1) is not marketing fluff: it is the sentence that keeps the whole architecture *outside* the MAS fund-management perimeter (§5.3). Any group copying the design must respect that boundary deliberately — the moment third-party capital enters, the RFMC/CMS and 13O machinery becomes mandatory, not optional.
2. **Entity separation is a feature, and directory blur is a bug.** The #33-00 cluster shows what happens when three entities share one suite and one brand without a public entity map: directories conflate them, RecordOwl pastes one mandate onto two UENs, and a counterparty cannot tell which entity "manages capital" (§2.3, §3.4). The design fixes this with a public entity directory — the single cheapest credibility asset a private group can buy.
3. **The registry codes are the first disclosure.** Bond Capital Group's 70201 code says consultancy; Bond Capital Partners' 64202 says investment holding. A private group's SSIC choices are its only mandatory public statement of what it does — choosing them accurately (and amending when the business changes, as BCG did in Aug 2024) is the foundation of honest registry hygiene.
4. **Singapore's value is the regime stack, not the licence.** Neither the real cluster nor the design needs a MAS licence; the value of Singapore is the holding economics plus the VCC/13O optionality (§6.5) — the same machinery the 2,000+ SFOs use. The design's Step 3 converts that optionality into a funded, tax-wrapped structure.
5. **Thin footprint is a choice — and it has a price.** The cluster's privacy envelope costs it verifiability: no bank, partner or regulator can distinguish "family capital group" from "paper shell" on the public record alone (§3.5). The design's substance plan (accounts, licensing map, entity directory) is the price of being *bankable* — a price the worked example treats as a competitive asset, not a compliance cost.

### 7.7 The Implementation Plan — Three Phases

The design is deliberately sequenced so that each phase *produces evidence* before the next phase spends money (the anti-thin-footprint discipline, mirroring the phased plans in [singapore_private_markets_guide.md](singapore_private_markets_guide.md) §8.5 and the sibling guide §8.7):

**Phase 1 — Foundation (months 0–6).** Incorporate Tan Holdings (64202) and Tan Advisory (70201) in Singapore (the BCG/BCP registry pattern, now deliberate); execute the Shenton Way tenancy and document group substance (local directors, corporate secretary, board minutes, group bank accounts with the SG banks — [banks_in_singapore_guide.md](banks_in_singapore_guide.md) §2). Produce the **licensing map** — the single document that states the proprietary-capital basis, each entity's MAS status, and the exact trigger points (third-party capital, fund vehicle) that would change it. Publish the entity directory.

**Phase 2 — Operating (months 6–18).** Launch the short-duration manager-selection programme: documented DD files for each Long/Short, Arbitrage, Macro and Quant allocation (the bond.sg public-sleeve model, now with audit trail). Close the first long-duration co-investments (PE/VC partnerships and a first real-estate deal — the private/real-estate-sleeve models) through Tan Holdings, each with a signed partnership/co-investment file. File the first annual returns on time (the registry hygiene the real cluster maintains).

**Phase 3 — The wealth layer (months 12–36).** Incorporate **Tan Family Opportunities VCC** and file the **13O application** with MAS (budget for processing time — the FO-scheme backlog reported in 2025 shows the regulator is capacity-constrained, per the private-markets guide §8.5); stand up the RFMC/exempt manager; deploy the three sub-funds; and complete the first **audited group accounts**. At that point the design has everything the real #33-00 cluster's public record lacks: an entity directory, a licensing map, filings, audits, and a brand that resolves to one documented set of legal entities.
