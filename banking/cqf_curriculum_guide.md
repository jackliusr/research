## 1. The Overview, the Integrity Rule and the Decoder

### 1.1 What This Guide Is

This guide is a **factbook about a published curriculum**. It answers one narrow question: *when a body awards a "Certificate in Quantitative Finance", what does that body say it teaches, how does it say it assesses, what does it charge, and what does it decline to publish?* It covers, for the Certificate in Quantitative Finance (CQF):

- **The awarding body** — who owns the credential and publishes the syllabus, and who delivers the teaching.
- **The published curriculum** — the optional primers, the six core modules and the complete published list of 21 advanced electives, each with the body's own stated scope (§3).
- **Assessment** — the three take-home exams, the pass mark, the capstone project, the optional distinction exam, the awards (§4).
- **The lifelong-learning and CPD structure** — the elective library, the Lifelong Learning library, the CPD position (§5).
- **Delivery, time commitment and the intake cycle** — as published, and no further (§6).
- **Eligibility** — what the body formally requires, and the finding that it publishes no formal entry requirement at all (§7).
- **Cost** — the published fee table, its currency, its dated bands, its inclusions, and its absences (§8).
- **The comparison set** — the university MFE/MQF route, the FRM, the CFA and the practitioner certifications, each sourced to its own publisher (§9).
- **Recognition** — what is published, what is a provider positioning label, and what could not be verified at all (§11).

The centre of gravity of this document is **§3**, the module factbook. It is the longest section by a wide margin, and deliberately so: every other section exists to make that module-by-module account usable. This is not an "is the CQF worth it" essay with a module list bolted on. It is the module list, with the surrounding apparatus needed to read it honestly.

### 1.2 The Integrity Rule

> **This guide describes published syllabi, module structures, assessment formats, eligibility, fees and CPD requirements — the material the awarding body itself publishes for candidates. It asserts nothing about a requirement, a fee, a pass mark or a recognition claim that the awarding body has not itself published. Where the body does not publish something, this guide records the absence rather than filling it.**

Four consequences follow, and they are why the rule is stated plainly at the top rather than buried:

1. **Every curriculum claim carries the page it came from and the date it was read.** The reading date throughout is **2026-09-28**, or the page's own stated applicability where it publishes one (for example, the fee table's stated applicability to the January 2027 program). A claim without a source is not a claim this guide makes.
2. **Absences are findings, not gaps to be filled.** Where the awarding body publishes no per-module lecture count, no topic weighting, no pass rate, no non-USD price and no annual CPD hour requirement, this guide says exactly that. It does not substitute a plausible estimate, an aggregator's summary, a reseller's number or a forum consensus.
3. **The provider's marketing is the dominant noise around this credential, and is treated as evidence of what the provider says, not of what is true.** Any outcome, salary, "most recognised", "world's leading", alumni-count, country-count, "master's-level" or degree-equivalence claim is attributed to the provider as the provider's own claim, dated, and labelled a marketing document. None is ever restated as fact, and none is ever used as a premise for a conclusion.
4. **Recognition is the claim most often overstated, so it is handled with the most care.** A taught professional qualification is not a university degree, and this guide keeps that distinction visible everywhere it matters — §2.3, §2.4, §11.2 and §14.5.

### 1.3 The Thesis Line

This guide's thesis, stated once here and again in the closing summary:

> **A curriculum is a list of things taught, not a list of things known.**

The distinction is not wordplay. A published curriculum tells a reader precisely what the provider intends to teach and how it intends to assess. It tells the reader nothing about whether any particular learner will absorb it, retain it, apply it under production pressure, or be hired for it. Every claim that a credential "produces" a quant, a modeller or a risk manager is a claim that has crossed the line between what is taught and what is known — and this guide does not cross it.

### 1.5 The Decoder — Reading a Published Curriculum

Quant-finance qualification marketing uses a compressed vocabulary, and the CQF's is compressed further because a six-month programme has to describe a large syllabus in short paragraphs. Decode it as follows.

| Term | What it actually means here | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The awarding body** | The entity that owns the syllabus and issues the credential. Here: the **CQF Institute**, described on the provider's own pages as "the global quant finance membership organization and the awarding body for the CQF program". | Whether the awarding entity is independent of the entity that sells and delivers the training. Here they sit inside the same corporate group — stated in §2.1, and it is a structural fact to weigh, not a scandal. |
| **The delivery partner** | An entity engaged to deliver or promote the qualification in a territory. The CQF's application form itself discloses arrangements for Mainland China and India. | Whether the candidate's contract, price and support sit with the awarding body or with the partner. The body publishes no partner-specific pricing (§8.4). |
| **The module** | A named block of the syllabus with a published scope paragraph. Six core modules are published. | Whether the module is examined, and whether the body publishes any topic weighting or lecture count for it. For the CQF: no weighting and no lecture count are published (§3.11). |
| **The elective** | A named specialist block drawn from a published library, of which the candidate must complete two. 21 titles are published. | Whether "access to all electives" is the same thing as "assessed on all electives". For the CQF it is not: two are selected for the qualification, all are available afterwards (§3.9, §5.1). |
| **The lecture and the weekly cycle** | The recurring taught unit. The only published weekly quantity is two live online lectures per week. | Whether a published "hours per week" figure exists. For the CQF it does not (§6.2) — only the primers carry published hour counts. |
| **The exam** | A summative assessment with a published format. Here: three take-home, open-book exams concluding modules 2, 3 and 4, two weeks each. | The exam format, the window, and the published pass mark — here 60% (§4.2). Not the pass rate: the body publishes none (§4.6). |
| **The final project (the capstone)** | The project assessment that follows module 6, taken from a select list of topics under a dedicated faculty supervisor. | Whether the project is one of "the exams". Here it is not: the body publishes three exams *plus* the capstone (§4.3). |
| **The pass mark** | The published threshold for a module exam. Here 60%, published on the assessment page. | Whether any other threshold — overall, distinction, project — is published. It is not (§4.2, §4.4). |
| **The CPD requirement** | The continuing-education position the body publishes. Here: at least 240 CPD points, claimable on completion of the full programme, one point per CPD hour, CPD UK certification. | Whether the body publishes an *annual* hour requirement to *retain* the credential. It does not (§5.5). Points earned are not points owed. |
| **The intake** | A cohort start date. The CQF publishes two intakes per year, January and June, with the next published intake on 27 January 2027. | Whether the intake date is the enrolment deadline or the teaching start, and whether a fee band is tied to it. The fee table's bands are tied to dates, not to intakes (§8.1). |

### 1.6 The Snapshot Date, and How to Read a Dated Claim

**This guide is a dated snapshot, compiled 2026-09-28 from the awarding body's own pages on that date. The awarding body's current syllabus governs, not this document.** The CQF is explicitly described by its provider as a syllabus reviewed periodically — the provider states this in two different ways on two different pages (see §15.5) — so a reader should assume that any item below may have changed since the reading date.

Three practical rules for using what follows:

1. **Prefer the page and the date in the same sentence.** Where this guide says "as published on the qualification page (read 2026-09-28)", that is the whole of the claim: it is what that page said on that date.
2. **Treat a fee band as a dated instrument.** The fee table is published as applicable to a named intake and is banded by named cut-off dates. Quoting the middle band a month later is a mistake even though the number was once correct.
3. **Treat the absence of published detail as a permanent feature until the body changes it.** "No per-module lecture count is published" is not a research failure — it is the state of the published record, and a reader planning study hours should not substitute someone else's estimate for it.

That does not make the credential illegitimate; it does mean a reader should not read independence into the arrangement that the published structure does not contain. Nothing in this guide turns on it one way or the other — but a reader comparing this credential against one awarded by a body independent of its training provider is comparing two different structures, and should know that. The Institute's scale claim ("over 40,000 members") is an **aggregate provider claim** and is recorded here as such. It is not a candidate number for any intake, and the body publishes no intake-level candidate statistics (§4.6).

### 2.2 Who Delivers It

Delivery is by **Fitch Learning**, and the provider's own application form discloses territory-specific arrangements. The guide records these in the words the form itself uses, because that form is the published source:

- "Fitch Learning has assigned **Golden Education** as the exclusive associate for delivering the CQF to Mainland China residents" — `cqf.com/start-your-cqf-application` (read 2026-09-28).
- "Fitch Learning has engaged **Times Edutech and Events Limited** for promoting the CQF to residents of India" — same page.

Two boundary statements follow, and the guide holds to both:

1. These are recorded **as the application form discloses them**, and no further commercial characterisation is drawn. Whether a given partner sets local pricing, handles local support or bears local contractual responsibility is not published on any page read, and this guide does not speculate.
2. **This guide publishes no price from any partner, reseller or aggregator.** The only prices in this document come from the awarding body's own fee page (§8) and its own membership page (§5.4). Partner-market pricing for Mainland China and India is an absence in the published record (§8.4).

### 2.3 The Form of the Qualification — and What It Is Not

As published on `cqf.com/about-cqf/program-structure/what-is-cqf` (read 2026-09-28), the CQF is:

- **online** — not campus-based;
- **part-time** — designed around a working professional's schedule;
- **taught** — live lectures, recorded lectures, tutorials and labs, not self-study examination;
- **examined and project-assessed** — three take-home exams plus a capstone project (§4);
- studied **over six months**;
- offered in **two cohorts per year: January and June**;
- **deferrable for up to three years at no extra cost** (published on `cqfinstitute.org/`, read 2026-09-28).

And what it is **not**, stated as a structural fact rather than a criticism:

- It is **not a university degree.** No university awards it. It is awarded by a membership institute that is, on the provider's own published description, part of a corporate training group (§2.1). There is no published academic credit, no published credit transfer mechanism, and no published articulation into a degree programme.
- It is **not a licence to practise anything.** It confers no regulated activity permission in any jurisdiction visited for this guide.
- It is **not a membership in itself.** The Institute publishes a free CQF Alumni tier, but the qualification and the membership are separate items with separate pages (§5.4).

A reader who wants the taught/professional versus academic distinction worked through as a general question — not specific to this credential — should read `banking/aml_certifications_exam_content_guide.md` §2.3, which draws the line between professional-body qualifications and academic awards in the AML context; the same line holds here.

### 2.4 The Framework Question

This is the single most overstated element of the CQF's public description, so it is stated in three parts. **First, what the awarding body does publish.** The provider describes the CQF as a "**master's-level** professional qualification" on multiple pages (for example `cqf.com/` and `cqf.com/about-cqf/cqf-journey/who-is-it-for`, both read 2026-09-28). That descriptor is the provider's own positioning language, applied by the provider to its own product.

**Second, what the provider does not publish.** No page read on `cqf.com` or `cqfinstitute.org` states that the CQF sits on any national qualifications framework — no **RQF**, **SCQF**, **EQF** or **Ofqual** register entry, and no accredited-degree status. A search for a CQF/framework-level statement returned only the provider's own pages and a third-party document upload dated 2019. **The placement could not be verified at the body that would grant it.** This guide therefore does not assert that the CQF is on any framework, and equally does not assert that it is not: it records that the awarding body publishes no such placement and the search found none.

**Third, the distinction that must not be collapsed.** The CQF carries a **CPD certification** — the programme's modules and electives are described as CPD accredited and the CPD page carries the **CPD UK** mark and links to that certification service (`cqf.com/cpd-accreditation`, read 2026-09-28). A CPD-provider certification is an accreditation of *training content for continuing-education purposes*. It is **not** an academic level, **not** a framework placement, and **not** evidence of degree equivalence. The two are different instruments, issued by different kinds of organisation, for different purposes. Section 11.3 returns to this, because conflating them is the most common way the CQF's level gets overstated.

> **The honest formulation, for any reader who needs one sentence:** the CQF is a taught, examined, project-assessed professional qualification whose provider calls it "master's-level" and whose continuing-education content is CPD-certified; no published placement on any national qualifications framework was found, and this guide does not supply one.

## 3. The Published Curriculum, Module by Module

This is the section the rest of the guide exists to support. Everything in it is a reading of what the awarding body publishes, on the pages named, on 2026-09-28. Where the body publishes a scope paragraph and nothing more granular, that is exactly what is reproduced — no topic weighting, no lecture count and no hour count is invented to fill the space (§3.11).

### 3.1 How the Programme Is Organised — Three Stages and Two Options

The provider describes the CQF journey as **three phases**, published on `cqf.com/about-cqf/program-structure/program-overview` (read 2026-09-28):

| Stage | As published | What it contains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Prepare** | Optional pre-programme **primers** | Mathematics, Python Programming and Finance primers, available on enrolment (§3.2) |
| **2. Get qualified** | "**six core modules and two advanced electives**", "completing **three exams and a final project**" | Modules 1–6 plus the candidate's two chosen electives, and the capstone (§3.3–§3.9, §4) |
| **3. Continue** | **Lifelong Learning library** | Permanent access on completion, at no additional cost (§5.2) |

The qualification itself can be taken on two published routes, as set out on `cqf.com/about-cqf/program-structure/cqf-qualification` (read 2026-09-28):

> **Option 1 — Full Program:** "The program can be taken in full by completing the six modules and two chosen electives in six months. This option provides you with instant access to all the materials and to Lifelong Learning."
>
> **Option 2 — Level I & II:** "Level I is comprised of modules one to three and your first two exams. Level II consists of modules 4 through 6, your last exam and final project, chosen electives and Lifelong Learning."

⚠ **The body describes the same split differently on its fees page, and this guide records the conflict rather than harmonising it.** `cqf.com/about-cqf/program-fees/fees` (read 2026-09-28) states:

> "**Level 1** consists of the Mathematics, Python Programming and Finance Primers & Modules 1-3"
>
> "**Level 2** consists of Modules 4-6 & Lifelong Learning."

The two published descriptions differ on one point a candidate would care about most: whether the primers sit **inside** Level 1. Both pages publish the same prices for Level 1 and Level 2 (§8.1), so the conflict is descriptive rather than financial — but a candidate buying "Level 1" is entitled to know whether that purchase includes the primers, and the awarding body's own two pages answer the question differently on the same reading date. This guide does not pick a winner; it quotes both and treats the qualification page's fuller wording as the description of the structure.

### 3.2 The Optional Primers

Published on `cqf.com/about-cqf/program-structure/preparation` (read 2026-09-28). The page is explicit that these are **optional** and are for delegates "from diverse backgrounds in finance, economics, business, mathematics, engineering, IT, and the sciences" — for those whose "skills in math, finance, or programming need refreshing, or if you have any gaps in knowledge". Access is **immediate upon enrolment** ("available upon enrollment" on the provider's programme page, read 2026-09-28; and enrolment steps on `cqf.com/about` state that primers and support options open after the initial payment). **All three primers carry published hour counts — the only published hour counts in the whole qualification (see §3.11).**

| Primer | Published duration | Published description | Published topics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mathematics Primer** | "**12 hours of intensive training**" | "covering all the essential math preliminaries you need to know, and will ensure that you are confident with the level of math needed for the qualification" | Calculus (functions and limits; differentiation and integration; complex numbers; functions of several variables); Differential equations (first order; second and higher order); Probability (PDF; cumulative distribution; expectation algebra; key discrete and continuous distributions including the normal; central limit theorem); Statistics (general summary statistics; maximum likelihood estimator; regression and correlation); Linear algebra (matrices and vectors; systems of linear equations; eigenvalues and eigenvectors) |
| **Python Programming Primer** | "this **8-hour primer**" | "Dive into Scientific Computing with Python. **Perfect for beginners**" | Python syntax (variables, basic data types, control flow, functions, file I/O); mathematical applications (the math library, numerical arrays, linear algebra, statistics, data plotting); good programming practices, documenting code, debugging |
| **Finance Primer** | "this **10-hour intensive primer**" | For "industry pros seeking a refresher or newcomers looking to start a career in financial services" | Macro economics; capital markets fundamentals; money markets; time value of money; equities; bonds; swaps; FX; derivatives; commodities |

That is an **unverifiable equivalence claim**: no university, no framework body and no accrediting organisation publishes any such mapping, and the comparison is made by the seller of the material. It is recorded here so a reader recognises it, and it is never repeated as a fact in this guide.

### 5.3 The CPD Position

Published on `cqf.com/cpd-accreditation` (read 2026-09-28):

- "All six modules and advanced electives on the CQF qualification have been **CPD accredited**."
- "CQF delegates can earn **at least 240 CPD points** on the program. These points can be claimed upon **completion of the full program**."
- Footnote: "**Applicable for CQF delegates that studied from the June 2021 cohort onwards.**"
- "individuals need to think in terms of **one CPD point per CPD hour**"; a CPD hour is time in "active learning" — "the time they spent in a lecture, tutorial, or Lab (minus any time for breaks)".
- The page carries the **CPD UK** certification mark and links to that certification service.

Three clarifications the guide insists on, because this is where CPD language is most often inflated:

1. **The 240 points are earned, not owed.** The published instrument credits learning completed *on the programme*. It does not create a recurring obligation (§5.5).
2. **CPD UK certification is a CPD-provider accreditation.** It certifies that the programme's content is eligible continuing-education activity. It is **not** an academic level, **not** a framework placement, and **not** a substitute for the "master's-level" question in §2.4. Conflating the two is the most common way this credential's level is overstated (§11.3).
3. **"At least 240" is a floor published by the body, not a measurement.** No page read states the actual CPD points a typical delegate claims. A reader should not convert "at least 240" into "240" (§14.2's undated-quote problem applies to this number too).

### 5.5 The Absence of an Annual CPD Requirement

**The awarding body publishes no annual CPD hour requirement to retain the CQF qualification.** No page read on `cqf.com` or `cqfinstitute.org` describes points owed, an annual declaration, a re-certification cycle, a lapsed-status rule or a maintenance fee attached to the qualification itself. The CPD page describes points *earned* on the programme; the membership page describes *optional* paid tiers.

This is a genuine structural difference from many credentials, and it is worth stating in both directions:

- **In favour:** there is no published maintenance obligation, and therefore no published maintenance cost, to budget for. Section 8's fee position is, as published, complete at enrolment.
- **Against over-reading it:** the absence of a published requirement is not a published statement that the credential is "lifelong and never expires". It is the absence of a statement. A reader who needs certainty should ask the Institute directly, and the honest note here is that the absence was checked and recorded, not resolved.

For the contrasting model — a credential whose CPD burden is published and recurring, and what that costs a firm to administer — see `banking/aml_certifications_exam_content_guide.md` §11.5 ("The CPD and Evidence-Record Burden a Firm Carries"). Section 11.5 of that guide is the repository's treatment of the maintenance burden as an operating cost; this guide does not re-derive it.

## 7. Eligibility, Prerequisites and the Maths Question

This is the section a prospective learner needs most, and the one most often answered from folklore rather than from the published record. It is therefore kept strictly to what the awarding body publishes, and the absences are stated as findings.

### 7.1 What the Body Formally Requires

**Finding: the awarding body publishes no degree requirement, no years-of-experience requirement, no entrance examination and no formal mathematics or programming prerequisite.**

What *is* published, on `cqf.com/about` (read 2026-09-28), in the "How to Enroll" block:

> "**Entry Requirements:** You must be **18+** to enroll on the CQF program. We recommend that you have at least an **A-Level or equivalent in mathematics**."

Read precisely, that is **one requirement and one recommendation**:

| Published statement | Status |
| --- | --- |
| "You must be 18+ to enroll" | A stated requirement |
| "We recommend that you have at least an A-Level or equivalent in mathematics" | A stated **recommendation** — the body's wording is "recommend", and no page read converts it into a condition of admission |
| A degree, in any subject | **Not published as a requirement** |
| Years of professional experience | **Not published as a requirement** |
| An entrance exam or test | **Not published** |

The enrolment procedure is published as a sequence (`cqf.com/about`, read 2026-09-28): submit the application; the application is "reviewed by our admissions team within 48 hours"; once accepted, one month to submit the fees; after the initial payment, access to the primers and support options.

### 7.2 What the Application Collects

Published on `cqf.com/start-your-cqf-application` (read 2026-09-28), the form collects:

- **Occupation**, from a published list that includes academic, actuary, consulting, derivatives, fund management, hedge funds, IT, quantitative analysis, risk management, structuring, **full time student**, trading, and other; **work history** ("Your work history will be reviewed as part of your application and helps us better understand your current skill set"); and **academic history**, to be "used to assess your skill set and suitability".
- **A mathematics checklist**, published verbatim as: "**Please indicate the areas of mathematics you have covered in the past (select all that apply) — Calculus / Probability / Differential Equations / Linear Algebra / Statistics / None of the above**".
- **Programming languages known** ("Please list the programming languages that you know"); a **personal statement**; and **Source of Funding** (Personal / Company).

**The maths checklist is the most useful single artefact in this section**, and its last option is the one that matters: the form offers "**None of the above**" as a selectable answer. The awarding body therefore publishes that a candidate may apply having covered **none** of calculus, probability, differential equations, linear algebra or statistics. Whether that application succeeds is governed by review against criteria the body does not publish — which is the honest limit of this section (§7.5).

### 7.3 What the Curriculum Assumes

The published curriculum assumes substantially more than the published requirement states. Section 3.10 works this through module by module; in one paragraph:

Module 1 begins with applied Itô calculus and stochastic calculus (§3.3); Module 4 dives into supervised learning including regression, k-nearest neighbours, support vector machines and ensembles (§3.6); Module 5 moves into deep learning, neural networks, natural language processing and reinforcement learning (§3.7); Module 6 requires the implementation and limitation analysis of interest-rate models and the handling of structural, reduced-form and copula credit models (§3.8). Several electives assume programming fluency, and one assumes Docker, databases and API-based execution (§3.9, items 8 and 16). The gap is therefore real and it is published in both directions: **the curriculum assumes a quantitative and programming foundation; the entry requirement does not require one.** This guide does not resolve that gap into an admissions probability, because the awarding body does not publish the criteria against which applications are reviewed.

### 7.4 The Primers — the Body's Own Answer to the Maths Question

The published remedy is the primer set (§3.2), priced into the programme fee (§8.2), with published hours of **12 + 8 + 10 = 30**. The Mathematics Primer's published topic list is the body's own statement of the ground it expects to cover: calculus, differential equations, probability, statistics and linear algebra.

### 7.5 The Question Most Often Answered From Folklore

Three versions of the maths question circulate, and all three are answered here from the published record only:

| The folklore version | What the published record supports |
| --- | --- |
| "You need a maths degree to get in." | **Not supported.** No degree requirement of any kind is published; the published admission route is a reviewed application with a stated 18+ requirement and a maths recommendation |
| "You need to be able to code already." | **Not supported as a requirement.** The form asks what languages a candidate knows and offers no threshold; the Python Primer is published as "Perfect for beginners", 8 hours |
| "They'll fail you on the maths in Module 1." | **Not published either way.** The body publishes no failure statistics, no pass rate, and no per-module difficulty assessment (§4.6). The published scope of Module 1 does make clear what the module starts from (§3.3) |

The one honest sentence for a prospective learner is this: **admission is by application review against criteria the awarding body does not publish, and the curriculum that follows assumes a foundation the awarding body does not require.** Any answer beyond that — including one this guide might be tempted to give — would be folklore with a source list attached.

### 11.5 How to Assess a Recognition Claim in the Field

Five checks, in order, for anyone meeting a recognition claim about any credential:

1. **Who is asserting it?** If the source is the provider or a party that sells the training, it is marketing — read it, but do not weigh it as evidence.
2. **Is it dated, and does it name a market?** "Widely recognized" without a market and a date is unmeasurable.
3. **Is a framework or regulator named?** If a claim of level exists, the framework authority should publish it. If no authority publishes it, the claim is a descriptor.
4. **Is it about the credential or about individuals?** Testimonials, alumni counts and salary surveys are about selected individuals, not about a credential's standing.
5. **What is the employer's actual instrument?** A bank's own job descriptions, competency frameworks and role profiles are the only published evidence of what that bank requires. For how those instruments work in a regulated enterprise, see `banking/aml_certifications_exam_content_guide.md` §11.1 and §11.2 — the same reasoning applies to a quantitative credential, and this guide does not re-derive it.

## 12. The Regulated-Enterprise and Banking Angle

### 12.1 How a Bank Actually Uses a Qualification of This Kind

A bank in a regulated market uses a professional qualification of this kind in four distinct places, and they should not be confused with one another:

| Use | What it is | What it is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hiring signal** | A line on a CV that a recruiter may weigh alongside a degree, a portfolio or an interview | A hiring requirement, unless the employer's own published job specification says so |
| **Competency framework entry** | An item mapped into a skills matrix for a role family, often as evidence of "technical knowledge" | A substitute for assessed, demonstrated competence in the role |
| **Training budget line** | An approved spend out of a departmental or individual development budget | A licence or a permission to perform any regulated activity |
| **Currency mechanism** | A device for keeping a team's knowledge current (here, the Lifelong Learning library, §5.2) | A re-certification requirement — the body publishes none (§5.5) |

For how a bank's compliance and risk functions actually expect credentials to be evidenced, and how a firm carries the CPD record burden, see `banking/aml_certifications_exam_content_guide.md` §11 — §11.1 (what the function expects), §11.4 (credentials, competence and fitness-and-propriety), §11.5 (the CPD and evidence-record burden) and §11.6 (the training budget and retention argument). This guide references that treatment rather than re-deriving it.

### 12.2 Credential Requirement Versus Competence Requirement

The distinction a bank has to keep straight when funding anything, in two sentences:

- A **credential requirement** is a gate: a hire cannot be made, a role cannot be filled, or an approval cannot be granted without the paper. It is verified by the presence or absence of the credential.
- A **competence requirement** is a capability: the work must be done to a standard — a model must price, a validation must hold, a control must operate. It is verified by evidence of the work, and no credential satisfies it on its own.

The CQF is, on everything published, a **knowledge and modelling credential**: three examined modules, a capstone project and 21 electives (§3, §4). It is not a licence and it carries no permission. Where a bank's real requirement is a competence — producing a defensible curve-building implementation, validating a pricing model, or operating a limit-monitoring system — the credential can support the competence and cannot replace it. This is the same argument the aml guide makes for compliance credentials in §11.4, and the same argument the repository's risk guides make about models themselves: a model is only as good as its validation, monitoring and governance. For the validation discipline, see `banking/risk_management_models_guide.md` §9 ("Model Risk Management: SR 11-7 and the Validation Discipline"), and for the modelling subject matter the CQF's Modules 2, 4 and 6 correspond to, see that guide's §4 (VaR and expected shortfall) and §10 (machine learning in risk).

### 12.3 No Regulator Requires the CQF

**No regulatory instrument was found in this pass that requires, recognises or names the CQF.** No bank regulator, prudential standard-setter or professional-body regime read for this guide publishes the CQF as a required or recognised qualification for any role. Three careful limits on that statement: it is a finding from the sources read on 2026-09-28, not an exhaustive regulatory survey; it does not mean a bank may not list the CQF in its own internal role profiles, because internal frameworks are not regulatory instruments; and it does not make the qualification valueless — it makes it **voluntary**, which is the same status the aml guide assigns to the credentials that are not referenced by a regulator (see `banking/aml_certifications_exam_content_guide.md` §11.3). For the Singapore supervisory context, in which a bank operating here would situate any training and competency policy, see `banking/mas_regulations_guidelines_guide.md`.

## 13. The Cymbal Bank Worked Example

### 13.1 The Scenario — Fictional, and Labelled as Such

Cymbal Bank's markets technology group has a pricing-library capability gap: the curve-building and rate-model choices inside the bank's derivative pricing library have drifted from the models the trading desks assume, and the last two model-validation findings on the library cite the same two causes — undocumented curve-construction choices and an ageing interest-rate model implementation that nobody on the team can explain the limitations of. Cymbal's model-validation lead has read `banking/risk_management_models_guide.md` §9 and is asking the standard question: is this a training problem, a hiring problem, or a governance problem? Two colleagues have asked to be funded for the CQF.

### 13.4 The Request Cymbal Declines

**The request:** fund the full CQF for a senior business analyst on Cymbal's trade-capture team, whose stated reason is that the CQF "would look good on my CV and help me move into a quant-adjacent role eventually". **Cymbal declines, for three reasons that come straight out of the published material:**

1. **No capability gap is identified that the published curriculum addresses.** Cymbal's identified gaps are curve construction, interest-rate model implementation and documentation discipline (§13.1, §13.3). The trade-capture role touches none of them, and the applicant's own reason is credential-acquisition, not capability.
2. **The cost is published and large** — US$22,995–24,995 USD for the Full Program (§8.1) — and the published inclusion of "CQF alumni network access" (§8.2) is a personal benefit that accrues to the individual rather than to Cymbal's library.
3. **The published time commitment is a real one.** The only published weekly quantity is two live lectures per week (§6.2), against a programme with three two-week exams and a capstone (§4). Cymbal cannot model the effect on the analyst's delivery, and the awarding body publishes no hours-per-week figure to help it try (§6.2).

**What Cymbal does instead:** funds the same colleague for a shorter, narrowly-scoped internal or vendor course on the specific problem in their own pipeline, and keeps the CQF decision open for a role where the published curriculum maps to a gap. The decline is not a judgement about the colleague or the credential; it is the application of the rule that **a credential is funded against a requirement, and Cymbal could not state the requirement.**

### 13.5 The Request Cymbal Approves, With a Condition Attached

**The request:** fund the full CQF for a quantitative developer on the pricing-library team — the person who owns the interest-rate model implementation the validation findings cite. **Cymbal approves, with one condition attached:**

> **Condition:** before enrolment, the developer writes a one-page study and delivery plan that names (i) the two published modules and one elective that map to the library's two identified causes — Module 6, Module 3, and the Numerical Methods elective on the published scopes (§13.3); (ii) one concrete deliverable, due within ninety days of the capstone's completion: a written implementation-and-limitations note on Cymbal's curve construction and rate-model choice, reviewed by the model-validation lead; and (iii) the published assessment windows the developer will work around — three two-week exams and a capstone (§4.1, §4.3). If the deliverable is not produced, no further CQF funding is approved in that financial year.

**Why the condition, and not a bare approval:** the credential answers only the first of Cymbal's three questions (§13.2). The condition is the mechanism by which Cymbal converts the other two into something it can see — a written deliverable, reviewed by the function that raised the finding (the SR 11-7-style validation discipline in `banking/risk_management_models_guide.md` §9). The condition also respects the published structure: Cymbal is not inventing an assessment, it is attaching a bank-side deliverable to a programme whose published assessment is a capstone project on "real-world industry scenarios" (§4.3). **Financing, as the published options allow:** the application form publishes "Source of Funding: Personal / Company" (§7.2), so a company-funded enrolment is a published route. - **The factbook is usable, not decorative.** Working §3 against §13.1 produced a narrow mapping (Modules 1, 3, 6 and one elective) and an equally important negative finding (Modules 4 and 5 are irrelevant to this gap).
- **The credential's published detail supports *what will be taught* and nothing else.** Cymbal had to supply its own requirement, its own deliverable and its own review.
- **One approval and one decline came out of the same published material.** Neither decision required a claim this guide could not source; both required Cymbal to state its own requirement first.

## 14. The Anti-Patterns

Each entry: **symptom → cause → guardrail.** The guardrails are the ones this guide's own method supplies.

### 14.1 A Curriculum Summary Treated as a Syllabus

- **Symptom:** someone says "the CQF covers derivatives, risk and numerical methods" and a decision is taken on that basis.
- **Cause:** a summary written for one purpose (a CV line, a repository's education paragraph) is reused as a specification. The repository's own instance is `technology/quantitative_developer_skillset_guide.md` L738 (§10.2, Finding 2) — three words that describe perhaps four of six core modules.
- **Guardrail:** go to the module list. §3 is what a syllabus summary cannot replace, and the published module titles take ten minutes to read.

### 14.2 An Undated Module List, Quoted as Current

- **Symptom:** a syllabus, a fee or an eligibility rule is quoted with no date, in a document that will outlive it.
- **Cause:** the awarding body reviews its syllabus periodically — published in two different formulations as "every three months" and "quarterly" (§15.5) — and publishes no changelog, so an old list stays plausible indefinitely.
- **Guardrail:** date every claim and name the page (`what-is-cqf`, read 2026-09-28). Prefer an absence to an estimate.

### 14.3 A Provider Outcome or Salary Claim Repeated as Fact

- **Symptom:** "the CQF is the world's leading master's-level quant qualification" or an alumni-count, salary or career-outcome figure appears in a decision document without a label.
- **Cause:** provider marketing is written to be quotable, and the CQF's is a dense example (§11.1) — superlatives, aggregate counts, an equivalence claim, and testimonials from individuals at named institutions.
- **Guardrail:** attribute it to the provider, date it, label it a marketing document, and never reason from it. §15.3 is the register of every such claim in this guide.

### 14.4 The Credential Bought for the CV Rather Than the Capability

- **Symptom:** an application for funding whose stated reason is the credential's appearance, with no capability named (§13.4).
- **Cause:** a credential is easy to buy and hard to prove. The published price is knowable; the outcome is not.
- **Guardrail:** require the applicant to name (i) the published modules that map to a named gap and (ii) a deliverable produced afterwards. If neither can be named, the funding request is a personal-development request and should be assessed as one.

### 14.5 A Taught Qualification Assumed to Be a Degree, or Placed on a Framework

- **Symptom:** "it's basically a master's", or a framework level asserted for the CQF.
- **Cause:** the provider's own "master's-level" descriptor, plus a common conflation of the CPD certification with an academic level (§2.4, §11.2, §11.3).
- **Guardrail:** state the three facts separately — this is a taught professional qualification awarded by a membership institute; the provider describes it as "master's-level"; no framework placement was found published, and the placement could not be verified at the body that would grant it.

### 14.6 The Maths-Prerequisite Question Answered From Folklore

- **Symptom:** "you need a maths degree", or "you need to code already", or "they fail most people in Module 1".
- **Cause:** the published record is genuinely thin on the point — no formal prerequisite, no published pass rate, no published criteria for the application review (§7) — and folklore rushes in to fill a real gap.
- **Guardrail:** answer from the published record and stop there: 18+ published; mathematics "recommended"; a maths checklist on the form whose last option is "None of the above"; a 12-hour Mathematics Primer sold for exactly this gap; and a curriculum whose Module 1 scope makes the real starting point visible (§3.3, §3.10).

### 14.7 The Anti-Pattern Behind the Anti-Patterns

Every entry above is a version of one error: **treating a description of a product as a description of an outcome.** A module list, a fee table, a recognition label and a testimonial are all descriptions of an offering. None of them is a description of what will happen to the person who buys it. That is why the Integrity Rule sits at the top of this guide rather than in an appendix, and why the absences are recorded as findings rather than quietly filled. The published record about the CQF is rich on what is taught and assessed, and silent on what is learned, applied, retained and hired for. A guide that respected only the first half of that would be accurate about a syllabus and misleading about a life. The discipline of the second half — recording the absence — is the whole difference.

### 16.4 The Closing Summary

The CQF is published as a **six-month, online, part-time, taught professional qualification** — six core modules, a choice of two from a published library of 21 electives, three take-home open-book examinations at a published 60% pass mark, a capstone project, an optional distinction exam, and permanent access to a lifelong-learning library that the provider describes in far larger numbers than the programme itself. It is awarded by the CQF Institute, delivered by Fitch Learning, priced in USD in three dated bands, and it publishes no framework placement, no pass rate and no annual CPD obligation.

What this guide has tried to do is hold the published record exactly: every module scope quoted from the page it came from and dated; every elective listed as published; every fee dated to its band and its intake; every marketing claim labelled and dated; every absence — the pass rate, the per-module weighting, the non-USD price, the framework placement, the employer-side source — recorded as an absence rather than filled.

On "is it worth it", the honest answer is an analysis with its limits stated, not a verdict. A curriculum description tells a reader what will be taught and what will be assessed. It does not tell them whether they will learn it, retain it, apply it, or be hired for it. No credential confers competence, and no price is a proxy for one. Where a reader has a named capability gap that a published module scope addresses — as Cymbal Bank's pricing-library team did with Module 6 and the Numerical Methods elective in §13 — the factbook above is enough to make a defensible decision. Where they do not, it is not, and no amount of further reading about this credential will change that. **a curriculum is a list of things taught; it is not a list of things known.**
