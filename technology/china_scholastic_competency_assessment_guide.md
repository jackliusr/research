## 1. Verification: What "CSCA" Actually Is

### 1.1 The Short Answer

**"China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA)" is a real, verifiable assessment — but it is not what the name most naturally suggests.** It is **not** a domestic Chinese school examination, and it is **not** an alternative to the gaokao. It is a **standardized entrance/readiness test for international students applying for bachelor's (undergraduate) degree programs at Chinese universities**, introduced for the 2026/2027 admission cycle.

The test is organized by the **China Scholarship Council (CSC, 国家留学基金管理委员会)** — the agency that administers the Chinese Government Scholarship — and was developed jointly with experts from Chinese universities. Registration and official information are at **www.csca.cn**. The first global test session was held on **December 21, 2025**, and it became a required application document for bachelor's applicants from the **2026/2027 academic year**, with full mandatory status (including minimum score standards) planned for **2028**.

In short: CSCA is the new **international-track counterpart** to the domestic admission pipeline described in [Section 3](#3-the-broader-context-chinas-academic-assessment-landscape). A reader who encountered the name "CSCA" while researching *domestic* Chinese schooling (gaokao, 学业水平考试, 综合素质评价) has almost certainly run into a name collision, discussed in [1.5](#15-ambiguity-and-conflation-risks).

| **Official (university)** | Southwestern University of Finance and Economics (SWUFE) Chinese Government Scholarship Type A application guide (PDF) | CSCA transcript listed as a required application document for bachelor's applicants (2026/2027 cycle) |

| **Secondary (media)** | SICAS (study-in-China portal) articles, Oct 2025 and later | Describes CSCA as the "core evaluation tool" of the new 2026 study-in-China policy; confirms mandate for Chinese Government Scholarship universities |

No source was found describing a "China Scholastic Competency Assessment" as a **domestic** Chinese exam. The name consistently refers to the international-students test described above. That consistency across official and commercial sources is strong evidence the name is genuine and unambiguous in current usage (2025–2026).

### 1.3 The Chinese Name and the Naming Problem

The official Chinese name is:

> **来华留学本科入学学业水平测试** (*Láihuá liúxué běnkē rùxué xuéyè shuǐpíng cèshì*) — literally, "Academic Proficiency Test for Undergraduate Admission for International Students Studying in China."

Two naming traps deserve explicit flagging:

1. **The hypothesized name 中国学业能力测试 does not check out.** A reader (or an AI drafting tool) might guess the Chinese for "China Scholastic Competency Assessment" as *中国学业能力测试*. No evidence of an assessment by that name was found in this research. The verified name is the 来华留学本科入学学业水平测试 above.
2. **The English name is a near-translation of a *domestic* exam's English rendering.** The domestic high-school exam 学业水平考试 is routinely translated "Academic Proficiency Test" (see [3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams)). CSCA's Chinese name contains the same words (学业水平测试), which invites conflation between the international test and the domestic one. They are different examinations for different populations.

### 1.4 How CSCA Maps to China's Assessment Landscape

CSCA sits on the **international (来华留学, "study in China") track**, which runs parallel to the domestic track. The mapping:

| Track | Domestic students | International students |
|---|---|---|
| **Admission gate to university** | Gaokao 高考 (national college entrance exam) — [3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination) | **CSCA** (new, from 2026/27) — formerly ad hoc university tests / HSK + high school grades |
| **Academic proficiency / graduation exams** | 学业水平考试 (academic proficiency exams) — [3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams) | CSCA subject tests (math, physics, chemistry) serve an analogous "proficiency" function for admission |
| **Holistic evaluation** | 综合素质评价 (comprehensive quality evaluation) — [3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation) | University holistic review: CSCA score + transcripts + interviews (universities retain discretion) |
| **Language gate** | (Chinese is the students' language) | HSK (Chinese proficiency test) + CSCA "Professional Chinese" — [4.3](#43-csca-vs-hsk-related-but-different) |

CSCA therefore maps onto China's assessment landscape as the **international admission gate**, mirroring the gaokao's role domestically, borrowing the "proficiency test" concept from 学业水平考试, and feeding into the same holistic-review logic that 综合素质评价 embodies.

### 1.5 Ambiguity and Conflation Risks

Because the name is new (public awareness dates from roughly October 2025), several ambiguity risks must be stated plainly:

- **Age of the name.** "CSCA" did not exist as a widely known assessment before late 2025. Documents from before then that mention "CSCA" refer to something else (the acronym collides with unrelated organizations in other fields — e.g., various "Customer Service / State / Science" acronyms — and with the CSC itself, which is the *organizer*, not the exam).
- **Domestic vs international confusion.** Anyone researching Chinese schooling for domestic students and landing on "CSCA" has the wrong exam. The domestic exams are gaokao, 学业水平考试, 综合素质评价, and zhongkao ([Section 3](#3-the-broader-context-chinas-academic-assessment-landscape)).
- **Conflation with HSK.** Because CSCA includes a "Professional Chinese" paper, some summaries describe it as "replacing HSK." It does not, strictly: HSK is a language-proficiency certificate; CSCA is an academic-readiness test. The relationship is covered in [4.3](#43-csca-vs-hsk-related-but-different).
- **Commercial noise.** A cluster of third-party prep sites (csca.ai, csca.app, cscainternational.com, etc.) appeared quickly after the announcement. They are not official and some overstate or misstate details (see [6.1](#61-what-could-and-could-not-be-verified)).

## 2. CSCA in Depth: Purpose, Format, Administration

### 2.1 Purpose and Policy Rationale

Per the official embassy notice and university pages, CSCA's stated purpose is to:

1. **Standardize international undergraduate admission.** Replace the fragmented array of university-specific entrance tests, HSK-plus-grade requirements, and ad hoc evaluations with one unified, scientific, authoritative assessment standard.
2. **Assess language proficiency and academic readiness.** It measures both the Chinese (or English) academic language needed to follow university coursework and the foundational subject knowledge (math, physics, chemistry) expected of a first-year undergraduate.
3. **Serve as a reference for admission and scholarships.** Chinese universities use CSCA scores "as an important reference" in admission decisions and in evaluating applications for the Chinese Government Scholarship (CGS) and other scholarships.

The policy rationale fits the broader trajectory of Chinese higher-education internationalization: after two decades of rapid growth in international enrollments, policy has shifted from **quantity to quality** — tightening, standardizing, and professionalizing the admission of international students (see [2.8](#28-reform-context-why-csca-exists-now)).

### 2.2 Who Takes It, and at What Level

- **Population:** Non-Chinese citizens (international students) applying for **bachelor's (undergraduate) degree programs** at Chinese universities.
- **Not applicable to:** Domestic Chinese students (they take the gaokao); graduate (master's/PhD) applicants; non-degree or short-term exchange students. The SWUFE application guide, for example, lists CSCA transcripts as required "(限本科项目 / bachelor program only)".
- **Grade level equivalent:** The test assumes completion of senior secondary (high school) education — roughly the level of a 12th-grade / final-year secondary student (typically ages 17–19). The mathematics syllabus is aligned with senior-high-school mathematics content (see [2.4](#24-syllabus-content)).
- **Mandate timeline:**
  - **2025 (pilot):** First global test December 21, 2025. Registration opened November 2025.
  - **2026/27 cycle:** Bachelor's applicants must take CSCA *before* submitting applications; the transcript is a required application document (confirmed by SWUFE's 2026/27 guide; SICAS reports it became mandatory for Chinese Government Scholarship universities in 2026).
  - **2028:** CSCA becomes mandatory for **all** undergraduate applicants, and **minimum admission standards** will be established (per the official notice).

### 2.3 Test Subjects and Format

CSCA comprises **five test papers in four subject areas**, all **multiple-choice**, each scored **0–100**:

| Subject | Test language | Duration | Questions | Score range | Mandatory? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Professional Chinese — Humanities (文科中文) | Chinese | 90 min | 80 | 0–100 | For Chinese-taught programs (choose Humanities or STEM track) |
| Professional Chinese — STEM (理科中文) | Chinese | 90 min | 80 | 0–100 | Same as above |
| Mathematics | Chinese **or** English | 60 min | 48 | 0–100 | **Yes — all applicants** |
| Physics | Chinese **or** English | 60 min | 48 | 0–100 | STEM applicants: at least one of Physics/Chemistry |
| Chemistry | Chinese **or** English | 60 min | 48 | 0–100 | STEM applicants: at least one of Physics/Chemistry |

**Subject-selection rules (per official university pages):**

- **Mathematics** is mandatory for *every* applicant, whether applying to Chinese-taught or English-taught programs.
- **Professional Chinese** is mandatory for programs taught in Chinese, with two tracks: Humanities Chinese or STEM Chinese (track chosen per the intended major's requirements).
- **Exemptions from Professional Chinese:** applicants to programs taught entirely in English; and applicants to Chinese-language degree programs who hold a valid **HSK Level 4** score report (CUMT policy).
- **Physics / Chemistry:** applicants to science and engineering programs must submit a score for at least one of the two. Non-STEM majors (e.g., finance, marketing, accounting, English, sports training — per CUMT's list) may not need either.
- Test language for math/physics/chemistry is chosen per the language of instruction of the applied program; Chinese-taught programs use the Chinese versions, English-taught programs the English versions.

**Test format:** Initially **remote, home-based online testing**; on-site test centers (computer-based or paper-based) are being established gradually. The first on-site centers launched in **Vietnam and Thailand in December 2025**, marking the start of a hybrid online/on-site model. Sessions run simultaneously worldwide, with session timing rotated so that December/April/June sessions favor Asia-Pacific daytime and January/March sessions favor Europe/Africa/Americas daytime.

### 2.4 Syllabus Content

The official syllabus (2025 edition) is published at **csca.cn/testprep/examsv**. The GDUFS page reproduces the math and physics syllabi in detail; in summary:

- **Mathematics** (60 min, 48 single-choice items): *Sets and inequalities* (set operations, quadratic/fractional inequalities); *Functions* (domain/range, monotonicity, parity; power/exponential/logarithmic/trigonometric functions; arithmetic and geometric sequences; introduction to derivatives and elementary calculus); *Geometry and algebra* (analytic geometry of lines, circles, ellipses, hyperbolas, parabolas; vectors and complex numbers; introductory solid geometry); *Probability and statistics* (classical probability; mean, variance; basics of the normal distribution).
- **Physics** (60 min, 48 items): *Mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, optics, modern physics* — the standard senior-high physics core.
- **Chemistry** (60 min, 48 items): senior-high chemistry core (the full syllabus was not reproduced in the pages retrieved, but the structure mirrors math/physics).
- **Professional Chinese:** assesses the Chinese needed to follow university lectures and textbooks in either humanities or STEM tracks.

This is deliberately **high-school-level content** — a readiness test, not a competition exam. It resembles the domestic 学业水平考试 selective-subject papers in scope ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams)) rather than the gaokao's difficulty.

### 2.5 Scoring and Score Reporting

- Each subject is scored on a **0–100 scale**.
- **No universal passing score or cutoff exists yet.** The official notice states universities "will set their own admission criteria based on individual program requirements." ECUST's FAQ is explicit: "We do not set a fixed minimum score for the CSCA. The admission board will conduct a comprehensive review." Minimum standards are planned only from **2028**.
- **Percentile ranking:** universities can view each applicant's **percentile ranking among all global test-takers**, allowing comparison of an applicant against the international applicant pool.
- **Score release:** within **7 working days** for online and on-site computer-based tests; within **14 working days** for paper-based tests.
- Scores are used for admission and scholarship evaluation (including Chinese Government Scholarship review), alongside transcripts, recommendation letters, and other documents.

**Registration** (per the official notice):

1. Prepare a compliant photo, a valid government-issued ID, and a payment method (credit card).
2. Review exam date, format, and location.
3. Create an account and register online at **www.csca.cn** (an account is required before registering or making inquiries).

**Note on retakes:** multiple sessions per year exist, and candidates may take one or more subjects in the same session, but no official retake policy was found in the sources retrieved — see [6.1](#61-what-could-and-could-not-be-verified).

### 2.7 How CSCA Fits the Chinese Education System

CSCA is best understood by analogy to the domestic system:

- **It plays the gaokao's role on the international track.** Just as the gaokao is the near-universal admission gate for domestic students ([3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination)), CSCA is becoming the standardized admission gate for international undergraduates. Before CSCA, universities used their own entrance exams, HSK thresholds, and high-school transcripts, which made comparisons across applicants difficult — the same standardization problem the gaokao solved domestically in 1952/1977.
- **It borrows the concept of 学业水平考试.** The Chinese name literally includes 学业水平 (academic proficiency). Domestically, 学业水平考试 certifies that a student has mastered the high-school curriculum and, in reformed provinces, contributes selective-subject scores to gaokao admission ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams)). CSCA's subject papers perform an analogous proficiency check for international applicants.
- **It feeds holistic review, like 综合素质评价.** The 综合素质评价 (comprehensive quality evaluation, [3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation)) philosophy holds that a single score should not decide admission. CSCA scores are explicitly "a reference," with universities conducting comprehensive review (score + percentile + transcripts + interviews) — the same multi-criteria logic, applied to the international track.
- **It complements HSK rather than replacing the whole language system.** For Chinese-taught programs, HSK remains a widely required certificate (typically HSK 4–5) for admission, visa, and scholarship purposes; CSCA's Professional Chinese adds academic-language testing, and an HSK 4 certificate can substitute for Professional Chinese in some programs ([4.3](#43-csca-vs-hsk-related-but-different)).

### 2.8 Reform Context: Why CSCA Exists Now

CSCA is one output of a decade of reform in Chinese education, operating at three levels:

1. **Study-in-China quality reform (来华留学).** In 2018 the Ministry of Education issued quality standards for international-student higher education (《来华留学生高等教育质量规范（试行）》) and tightened admission requirements, reacting to rapid enrollment growth and quality concerns. CSCA (announced October 2025, effective 2026/27, fully mandatory 2028) is the culmination of that drive toward a "unified, scientific, and authoritative admission assessment standard" (SICAS's characterization of the 2026 policy).
2. **Domestic exam reform (gaokao reform).** The 2014–2025 gaokao reform ([3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination)) pushed Chinese assessment culture toward multiple criteria — subject choice, academic proficiency exams, and comprehensive quality evaluation — rather than a single exam score. CSCA's design (proficiency papers + holistic review + university discretion) mirrors this philosophy on the international track.
3. **Domestic curriculum reform.** The 2022 compulsory-education curriculum standards (新课标) and the competency-oriented 核心素养 (core competencies) framework reshaped what Chinese high-school graduates are expected to know — which is precisely the level CSCA's syllabus samples. Note: the 双减 (Double Reduction) policy ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams) note) targets private tutoring in compulsory education and does not directly govern CSCA, but the same "quality over pressure" discourse surrounds both.

### 2.9 The Application Flow Around CSCA

CSCA is one step inside a larger application machine. Understanding the flow clarifies where the score enters and who sees it:

1. **Register and sit CSCA** (www.csca.cn) — typically several months before application deadlines, using the designated sessions for your entry year ([2.6](#26-administration-schedule-fees-registration)).
2. **Receive scores** within 7–14 working days; universities can view each applicant's global percentile ([2.5](#25-scoring-and-score-reporting)).
3. **Apply to universities.** Two main Chinese Government Scholarship (CGS) routes exist, plus non-scholarship routes:
   - **Type A (国别项目 / agency-nominated):** nomination through the Chinese embassy or a dispatching organization in the applicant's home country; the embassy/agency publishes final results. SWUFE's Type A guide lists the CSCA transcript among the required documents for bachelor's applicants.
   - **Type B (university-direct):** application directly to the university's international-student office; universities shortlist candidates and report to CSC for final approval.
   - **Non-CGS:** direct application to universities (or via provincial scholarships), with CSCA requirements set per university.
4. **Scholarship review:** CSC and universities use CSCA scores (plus percentiles) alongside transcripts, recommendation letters, study plans, and interviews to award admissions and scholarships.
5. **Offer and enrollment:** universities issue pre-admission notices; final CGS results come from the dispatching authority; enrollment is subject to visa, health, and on-arrival checks.

Because CSCA is a **reference** (not a hard cutoff) until 2028, a strong score must be paired with a complete, high-quality application — and a weak score is not necessarily disqualifying at universities that conduct holistic review. The 2028 minimum-standards regime will change this calculus.

## 3. The Broader Context: China's Academic Assessment Landscape

This section covers the assessments CSCA's name most plausibly gets confused with, plus the international tests China participates in. It is the essential background for any family or observer trying to make sense of "China + assessment."

### 3.1 Gaokao 高考 — The National College Entrance Examination

- **What it is:** The annual, nationally coordinated but provincially administered university admission examination (全国普通高等学校招生统一考试), held every June 7–8 (some provinces continue to June 9–10 for additional subjects). It has run since its 1977 restoration (origins 1952) and is the single most consequential exam in Chinese life.
- **History:** Established 1952, suspended during the Cultural Revolution, restored in **1977** (first cohort: ~5.7 million candidates for ~277,000 places, ~4.8% acceptance). The 1999 mass expansion (高校扩招) and 21st-century enrollment growth took gross tertiary enrollment from single digits to **60.2% by 2023** ([5.2](#52-higher-education-access)). The current reform wave began with the 2014 State Council plan that produced the new gaokao.
- **Scale:** Roughly **12.9–13.4 million registered candidates** per year in 2023–2026 ([5.1](#51-gaokao-scale)).
- **Structure — the old "3+X" model:** Chinese (语文), Mathematics, and a foreign language (usually English), plus "X" = a comprehensive paper: 文科综合 (history, politics, geography) for arts students or 理科综合 (physics, chemistry, biology) for science students. This model, standard from the late 1990s, still applies in the last provinces not yet converted (as of 2025: Xinjiang and Tibet).
- **Structure — the new gaokao (3+1+2):** Reformed provinces use:
  - **3 common subjects:** Chinese, Mathematics, Foreign language — all students, scored on the raw national/provincial scale.
  - **1 preferred subject:** choose **Physics or History** — scored raw, defining the "science" or "humanities" track.
  - **2 re-selected subjects:** choose any **two of Chemistry, Biology, Politics, Geography** — scored via **grade-band conversion (等级赋分)**, not raw scores, to make scores comparable across subjects.
  - **Grade-band conversion (等级赋分):** the two re-selected subjects are converted from raw scores into grade bands (province-specific; commonly 5 grades subdivided into ~20+ levels, each mapped to a fixed score interval) before being added to the total, so that subject difficulty differences don't distort totals. Details vary by province.
  - Earlier reform provinces (Zhejiang, Shanghai, Beijing, Tianjin, Shandong, Hainan) use a "3+3" variant with wider choice.
- **Rollout (five phases, 29 provinces by 2025):**

| Phase | Provinces (first new-gaokao exam year) | Model |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (2014) | Shanghai, Zhejiang (2017) | 3+3 |
| 2 (2017) | Beijing, Tianjin, Shandong, Hainan (2020) | 3+3 |
| 3 (2018) | Hebei, Liaoning, Jiangsu, Fujian, Hubei, Hunan, Guangdong, Chongqing (2021) | 3+1+2 |
| 4 (2021) | Jilin, Heilongjiang, Anhui, Jiangxi, Guizhou, Guangxi, Gansu (2024) | 3+1+2 |
| 5 (2022) | Shanxi, Inner Mongolia, Henan, Sichuan, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Qinghai, Ningxia (2025) | 3+1+2 |

  By 2025, **29 of 31 mainland provincial units** had adopted the new model; Xinjiang and Tibet had begun reform preparations (Caixin, May 2025). Total score is typically 750 points (varies slightly by province).

- **Admission mechanics:** Candidates apply through 平行志愿 (parallel-preference) systems in sequential admission batches (early batch 提前批, bachelor batch 本科批, specialist batch 专科批), with provincial admission quotas per university. Admission is overwhelmingly determined by gaokao score; 综合素质评价 files and university assessments play supporting roles (see [3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation)). Special channels include:
  - **强基计划** (Strong Foundation Plan, since 2020): basic disciplines (mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, history, philosophy, Chinese) — typically 85% gaokao + 15% university assessment, with the 综合素质 file consulted.
  - **综合评价录取** (comprehensive-evaluation admission): concentrated in coastal provinces; gaokao score + university written/interview + quality record ([3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation)).
  - **Art/sports admissions** (艺术类/体育类): professional tests plus gaokao at reduced weight.
  - **专项计划** (special programs): additional quotas for rural and poverty-stricken regions.
  - **职教高考 / 对口招生** (vocational gaokao): a separate vocational-track examination for secondary-vocational (中职) graduates — an increasingly prominent "second track" in the 2020s as policy pushes vocational education.

### 3.2 学业水平考试 — Academic Proficiency Exams

- **What it is:** 普通高中学业水平考试 (often shortened to 学考), the high-school academic proficiency examination — the domestic "academic proficiency test" whose English rendering collides with CSCA's Chinese name ([1.3](#13-the-chinese-name-and-the-naming-problem)).
- **Roles:**
  - **Qualifying exams (合格性考试):** All high-school subjects; passing certifies completion of the high-school curriculum and is a **graduation requirement** (the diploma, 毕业证, plus the gaokao, historically gate university admission).
  - **Selective exams (选择性考试):** In new-gaokao provinces, the two "re-selected" subjects' scores are produced by these exams and **count directly into the gaokao total** via grade-band conversion.

| Dimension | 合格性考试 (Qualifying) | 选择性考试 (Selective) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Certify curriculum completion; graduation requirement | Produce admission scores for the new gaokao |
| Subjects | All senior-high subjects | Only the student's two re-selected subjects (chem/bio/politics/geography) |
| Scoring | Pass/fail (合格/不合格) or A–D grades | Raw score converted via 等级赋分 into a gaokao contribution |
| When | Multiple sessions per year | Once per year, aligned with the gaokao schedule |
| Role | Diploma gate | Adds directly to the gaokao total (new model) |

- **Who takes it:** All senior-high (grades 10–12) students, plus adults via the 社会考生 route; held multiple times per year per province.
- **Why it matters beyond graduation:** It is a formal "academic proficiency" record on the 综合素质档案, referenced in some admissions; and it is the closest domestic analogue to CSCA's subject papers in concept and content level ([2.4](#24-syllabus-content)).
- **Reform note (双减, Double Reduction):** The July 2021 "Double Reduction" policy (《关于进一步减轻义务教育阶段学生作业负担和校外培训负担的意见》) banned for-profit tutoring in core K-9 subjects and cut homework loads. It does not govern gaokao or CSCA directly, but it is the defining 2020s domestic-education policy and shapes public discourse about exam pressure. The 2022 compulsory-education curriculum standards (新课标) — competency-based, emphasizing 核心素养 — are the curricular counterpart.

### 3.3 综合素质评价 — Comprehensive Quality Evaluation

- **What it is:** A structured record (综合素质档案) of a student's non-exam qualities, mandated as part of gaokao reform. Provinces differ slightly, but the standard framework covers **five domains** — the "five educations" (五育): 德育 (morality), 智育 (academic/intellectual), 体育 (physical health), 美育 (aesthetics), 劳育 (labor/social practice).
- **How it is used:**
  - **Reference in gaokao admission:** universities may consult it when candidates are otherwise tied; its weight is officially "reference," not a scored component in most provinces.
  - **综合评价录取 (comprehensive-evaluation admission):** A dedicated admission channel at roughly 90+ universities (concentrated in Zhejiang, Shanghai, Shandong, Jiangsu, Guangdong) combining gaokao score (~60–85%), university assessment (written/interview), and the quality record.
  - **强基计划:** basic-discipline admissions also review the quality file.
- **CSCA parallel:** For international students, the CSCA score plays the role of the "academic" pillar while universities review transcripts, recommendation letters, and interviews — the same multi-dimensional logic ([2.7](#27-how-csca-fits-the-chinese-education-system)).

### 3.4 Zhongkao 中考 — The Senior High Entrance Exam

- **What it is:** The 初中毕业暨高中招生考试 — the compulsory-education completion and senior-high admission exam, administered by cities/prefectures (not nationally unified), typically taken in June of grade 9 (age ~15).
- **Subjects:** Chinese, Mathematics, English, plus physics, chemistry, politics/history, and (in many cities) physical-education tests; some cities now include art and computer literacy.
- **Stakes:** It sorts students into academic senior high (普通高中) vs. vocational senior high (职业高中/中专). Official policy (职业教育法 2022, vocational-streaming guidance) targets a roughly 50/50 split between academic and vocational tracks, though actual ratios vary by city. It is often called "more stressful than gaokao" by Chinese families because it happens at age 15 and shapes the gaokao track.
- **Relevance here:** zhongkao is the earliest gate in the domestic pipeline; international applicants are not subject to it, but observers comparing systems should know it exists between compulsory education and the gaokao.

### 3.5 International Assessments: PISA and TIMSS

- **PISA (OECD, 15-year-olds, every 3 years):**
  - **PISA 2018:** China participated via the **B-S-J-Z (Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang)** sample and ranked **#1 in all three domains** — reading **555**, mathematics **591**, science **590** (OECD mean ≈ 487–489). This result received enormous attention and some methodological skepticism: the sample covers four affluent coastal regions, not the whole country, and sampling was not fully transparent (see the Fordham Institute critique found in research).
  - **PISA 2022:** **Mainland China did not participate** (B-S-J-Z absent); Hong Kong, Macao, and Chinese Taipei did, remaining in the leading group.
  - **PISA 2025:** administered 2025; **results due December 2026** (OECD). Whether mainland China (and with which sample) participated was **not verifiable** in this research — flagged in [6.1](#61-what-could-and-could-not-be-verified).
- **TIMSS (IEA, grades 4 and 8, math and science, every 4 years):**
  - **Mainland China has never participated in TIMSS** (general knowledge; no contrary evidence found in this research). Hong Kong, Macao, and Chinese Taipei participate; Hong Kong consistently ranks near the top, with Singapore typically first in recent cycles (e.g., TIMSS 2023).
  - The practical takeaway: China's international benchmarking presence is **PISA via B-S-J-Z**, with all the representativeness caveats that entails.

### 3.6 The Policy Ecosystem (Double Reduction, New Curriculum, Five Educations)

Three 2020s policy streams shape everything above — and explain the reform atmosphere in which CSCA was born:

- **双减 (Double Reduction, July 2021).** 《关于进一步减轻义务教育阶段学生作业负担和校外培训负担的意见》 banned for-profit tutoring in core K-9 subjects, capped homework, and pushed schools to provide after-school care. Its assessment consequence: reduced reliance on exam-preparation cramming in compulsory education and renewed public debate about exam pressure — even as the gaokao remains decisive. It does not directly govern CSCA (which sits on the international track), but it is the defining domestic-education policy of the decade and the context for every discussion of Chinese exam culture.
- **新课标 (New Curriculum Standards, 2022).** The 2022 义务教育课程方案和课程标准 reoriented compulsory education around 核心素养 (core competencies) — integrated skills rather than memorized knowledge — with corresponding changes in how schools assess internally. Senior-high curriculum standards (2017 edition, 2020 revision) set the competency framework that the gaokao and 学业水平考试 now sample. CSCA's syllabus (senior-high content, readiness-oriented, single-choice items) is implicitly aligned with this framework.
- **五育并举 (Five Educations in Parallel).** Official doctrine since 2018–2019: develop 德育 (morality), 智育 (intellectual), 体育 (physical), 美育 (aesthetic), and 劳育 (labor) education together. 综合素质评价 ([3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation)) is its assessment instrument; the "德智体美劳" framework appears in admission records and policy language around holistic evaluation — the same multi-dimensional logic CSCA feeds into on the international track.

Together these mark a shift from **single-score selection toward multi-dimensional evaluation** — a shift CSCA replicates internationally, even though it is itself a conventional scored test.

### 3.7 Assessment Landscape at a Glance

| Assessment | Chinese name | Level | Purpose | Format | Role in admissions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CSCA** | 来华留学本科入学学业水平测试 | Post-secondary entry (international) | Standardized readiness + Chinese academic language for international bachelor applicants | MCQ, online/on-site, 5 papers | Required application document for international undergraduates (2026/27+); university-set thresholds |

## 4. Practical Considerations for Families and International Observers

### 4.1 For International Applicants: The CSCA Pathway

If you (or your child) are an international student targeting a Chinese bachelor's program from the 2026/27 cycle onward:

**Key dates for the 2026/27 cycle (as announced):**

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| CSCA sessions designated for September 2026 entry | December 21, 2025; January 25, 2026 |
| Regular CSCA sessions from 2026 | January, March, April, June, December |
| Typical university application windows | Winter–spring (roughly December–June, university-specific); CGS Type A online submissions for the 2026/27 cycle were due around February 2026 in some embassies (deadlines vary by country) |
| Enrollment | September 2026 (fall intake) |

1. **Confirm the requirement early.** Check the target university's admission brochure (招生简章) for its 2026/27 CSCA requirements — subject mix, language versions, and any minimum-score policy (many, like ECUST, currently have none). The list of Chinese Government Scholarship universities is published on **campuschina.org**.
2. **Register at www.csca.cn** (official portal; account required). Have a compliant photo, valid passport/ID, and payment card ready.
3. **Pick the right sessions and subjects.** For September 2026 entry, the designated sessions were December 21, 2025 and January 25, 2026; from 2026 there are five sessions/year (Jan, Mar, Apr, Jun, Dec) — plan around university deadlines (applications typically open winter–spring, e.g., March–June for many scholarship tracks). Take **Mathematics (always)** plus **Professional Chinese** (if the program is Chinese-taught) plus **Physics and/or Chemistry** (if STEM, per university rules).
4. **Choose test language deliberately.** Math/Physics/Chemistry are offered in Chinese or English — match the program's language of instruction. Note the Chinese versions and English versions of the same subjects are separate papers; an English-version score does not demonstrate Chinese academic ability.
5. **Prepare from the official syllabus** (csca.cn/testprep/examsv) — content is senior-high level. Official-adjacent materials (e.g., the BLCUP mock-test book "CSCA 轻松过") and third-party prep sites exist; treat the latter with care ([6.1](#61-what-could-and-could-not-be-verified)).
6. **Don't drop HSK.** Many Chinese-taught programs still require an HSK certificate (commonly HSK 4–5) for admission, scholarship, and visa purposes. CSCA's Professional Chinese paper does not automatically replace HSK except where a university explicitly says so (e.g., CUMT accepts HSK 4 in lieu of Professional Chinese for Chinese-language programs — the reverse substitution).
7. **Budget:** ¥450 (one subject) or ¥700 (two or more subjects) per session; no official fee waivers were found. Add HSK fees and application fees separately.
8. **Expect holistic review.** A strong CSCA score is necessary but may not be sufficient: universities combine scores, percentiles, transcripts, recommendation letters, and interviews. There is currently no universal cutoff ([2.5](#25-scoring-and-score-reporting)).

### 4.2 How Chinese Assessments Are Used in Admission and Transcripts

- **Domestic track:** The gaokao score is the dominant currency; the 综合素质评价 file and 学业水平考试 records appear on the 综合素质档案 and are consulted in tie-breaks, 综合评价录取, and 强基计划 ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams), [3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation)). "Transcripts" in the Western sense are secondary — the gaokao report card (成绩单) is what matters.
- **International track:** Universities now require the CSCA transcript alongside notarized high-school diplomas and transcripts, language certificates, study plans, and (for graduate programs) recommendation letters. The SWUFE guide shows CSCA listed explicitly among required documents for Chinese Government Scholarship Type A bachelor applicants.
- **Scholarships:** CSCA scores are used in Chinese Government Scholarship evaluation; a good percentile rank strengthens scholarship applications.

| Channel | Route | CSCA role |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese Government Scholarship — Type A | Embassy/agency nomination (bilateral and special programs) | Required document (bachelor's); score reviewed by university and CSC |
| Chinese Government Scholarship — Type B | Direct university application | Required document; university shortlists, CSC approves |
| Provincial/municipal scholarships | University or provincial offices | University-dependent |
| University scholarships | University international office | University-dependent; scores often used in merit review |

Exact eligibility rules vary by country and university — always check the local Chinese embassy and campuschina.org.

### 4.3 CSCA vs. HSK: Related but Different

| Dimension | HSK | CSCA |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | 汉语水平考试 (Chinese Proficiency Test) | China Scholastic Competency Assessment |
| What it measures | Chinese language proficiency (listening, reading, writing; spoken test Hskk separately) | Academic readiness: subject knowledge + academic Chinese |
| Levels | 1–6 (new HSK 3.0 restructures to 1–9 with banding) | Single test, 0–100 per subject |
| Who needs it | Anyone needing a Chinese-language certificate (study, work, visa) | International bachelor's applicants to Chinese universities (2026+) |
| Relationship | Can exempt Professional Chinese in some programs (HSK 4, per CUMT); often still required for Chinese-taught programs | Does not certify general language proficiency; complements HSK |

Practical rule: **plan for both** unless the target university explicitly waives one.

### 4.4 Chinese Assessments vs. IB, A-Levels, and SAT

| Dimension | Gaokao | CSCA | IB Diploma | A-Levels | SAT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Origin / owner | China (provincially administered) | China Scholarship Council (CSC) | IBO (Switzerland) | UK exam boards (CIE, Pearson, AQA, etc.) | College Board (US) |
| Age/level | End of senior high (age ~18) | Post-secondary entry (international applicants) | Ages 16–19 (2-year diploma) | Ages 16–19 (subject-based, 2-year) | Ages 16–18 (college entry) |
| Structure | 3+1+2 or 3+X; comprehensive, all-subject | 5 MCQ papers: Math (all) + Professional Chinese + Physics/Chemistry | 6 subjects + TOK + EE + CAS | 3–4 subjects, deep, modular | Evidence-based reading/writing + math (+ optional essay) |
| Scoring | ~750-point total, percentile-based provincial ranking | 0–100 per subject + global percentile | 45-point diploma (7 per subject + core) | A*–E grades per subject | 400–1600 |
| Role in admissions | Near-decisive for domestic admission | Required reference document for international undergrad admission | Accepted worldwide, incl. many Chinese universities for intl. applicants | Accepted worldwide; UK-centric; used by Chinese universities for intl. applicants | US-centric; some Chinese intl. programs accept as alternative evidence |
| Retakes | Once a year (next June) | 5 sessions/year | Full diploma cycle | Per exam session (Jan/May/Jun/Oct) | 7/year |
| Language | Chinese | Chinese or English (subjects) | English (or school language) | English | English |
| Difficulty character | High-stakes, one-shot, norm-referenced, extreme competition | Readiness-level (senior-high content), criterion-referenced with percentile context | Breadth + critical thinking, coursework + exams | Depth per subject, exam-heavy | Aptitude-style, content-light, coaching-sensitive |

Key comparative takeaways:

- **Gaokao** is the hardest to "game" and the most consequential; **CSCA** is deliberately modest (high-school content) and currently threshold-free — a gate, not a ranking gauntlet.
- Chinese universities accept **IB/A-Level/SAT** results from international applicants as evidence of academic ability; from 2026/27, **CSCA sits alongside** those credentials (universities differ on whether CSCA can be waived for strong IB/A-Level holders — verify per university; no blanket waiver was found in this research).
- For families comparing systems: the gaokao's role ≈ the role CSCA is *beginning* to play for internationals, while IB/A-Levels/SAT remain optional alternative credentials in the same application.

### 4.5 Reading Chinese Assessment Results from Abroad

- **Percentiles, not cutoffs:** because most universities currently set no fixed CSCA minimum, a percentile rank against the global applicant pool is the most informative number ([2.5](#25-scoring-and-score-reporting)).
- **Province-level reality for domestic exams:** gaokao scores are not comparable across provinces (different papers or conversion, different quotas). When reading Chinese press about "admission rates," always ask which denominator is used (see [5.2](#52-higher-education-access)).
- **Language of evidence:** official CSCA materials are in Chinese and English; the most authoritative per-university statements are on university international-student pages and in admission brochures, not on third-party prep sites.

### 4.6 Application Document Checklist

Based on the SWUFE Type A guide (representative; universities vary slightly), a bachelor's application typically bundles:

| # | Document | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Passport photo (≤6 months) | Specs per application system |
| 2 | Passport bio page | Valid government-issued ID |
| 3 | Notarized highest diploma | E.g., high-school graduation certificate |
| 4 | Full transcript of highest education | Notarized/translated as required |
| 5 | Study plan / personal statement | ≥200 words for bachelor's (SWUFE example) |
| 6 | Recommendation letters | Usually required for graduate programs; sometimes for bachelor's |
| 7 | Language certificate (if any) | HSK for Chinese-taught; TOEFL/IELTS for English-taught programs |
| 8 | **CSCA transcript** | **Bachelor's programs only; required from 2026/27** |
| 9 | Physical examination record | 外国人体格检查记录 |
| 10 | Non-criminal record | 无犯罪记录证明 |
| 11 | Other documents | Portfolio, awards, financial proof — university-dependent |

All documents must be in Chinese or English (otherwise with notarized translations). Application fees: none for CGS Type A at SWUFE; other routes vary.

### 5.2 Higher Education Access

- **Gross tertiary enrollment ratio:** **60.2% in 2023** (Ministry of Education, official — up 0.6 pp from 2022; the 14th Five-Year Plan target was reached ahead of schedule). This is the "~60% to higher education" figure — it is a *gross* ratio of the age cohort, not a gaokao pass rate.
- **Total students in higher education:** over **47 million** (2023, MOE); ~3,074 higher-education institutions.
- **Expansion trajectory:** gross tertiary enrollment rose from ~3.4% (1990) to **60.2% (2023)** — one of the fastest massifications of higher education in history, driven by the 1999 expansion and 21st-century growth (Yicai: 3,510 higher-ed students per 100,000 people in 2022, versus 326 in 1990). This is why "admission rate" discourse shifted from scarcity (4.8% in 1977) to access (a majority of the cohort).
- **What "admission rate" means matters:** media frequently cite "~80–90% of gaokao candidates get a place" (counting 专科, two-to-three-year vocational colleges) or "~40–50% get 本科 (four-year bachelor's)" — both true for different denominators. The "50–60%" figure sometimes quoted corresponds roughly to four-year bachelor admission in some provinces, and to gross enrollment nationally; treat any single number as an approximation. Provincial rates vary widely (Tianjin/Beijing/Shanghai far higher; Henan/Guangdong among the most competitive per capita).
- **Projection:** with registrations falling and university places rising, acceptance rates are projected to keep climbing through 2026–2027 (media projection — flag).

### 5.3 International Students in China

- **2018:** ~**492,000** international students in China (MOE statistics, widely cited).
- **2019–2022:** numbers declined sharply during the pandemic (border closures); official detailed statistics were published less consistently after 2018 — exact trough figures were **not verified** in this research.
- **2023 onward:** a policy-driven recovery ("study in China" re-opened, quality-first standards) — but no verified official count for 2023–2025 was located in this research. **Flag:** treat any specific post-2019 figure with caution.
- Relevance: CSCA targets exactly this population (international bachelor's applicants), and the 2026/2028 mandate is designed for the post-recovery intake.

### 6.2 Recommended Reading of This Guide

- **If your interest is international study in China:** Sections [2](#2-csca-in-depth-purpose-format-administration) and [4.1](#41-for-international-applicants-the-csca-pathway) are the core; re-verify the schedule and per-university requirements against csca.cn and the target university's brochure, because the policy is young and evolving.
- **If your interest is domestic Chinese education:** [Section 3](#3-the-broader-context-chinas-academic-assessment-landscape) is the correct map; CSCA is only tangentially relevant (as the international mirror of the gaokao's role).
- **If you encountered "CSCA" in an older document (pre-2025):** it almost certainly refers to something else; do not retro-fit this guide's content onto it ([1.5](#15-ambiguity-and-conflation-risks)).
- **Time-stamp the information:** this guide is a snapshot as of **August 2026**. The 2026–2028 transition (five sessions/year; 2028 minimum standards; university policies hardening) will change details. Check dates on any source you rely on.

### 6.3 Verification Checklist for Readers

1. Read the official notice/FAQ: **www.csca.cn** (registration + syllabi at csca.cn/testprep/examsv).
2. Read the target university's international-admission brochure (招生简章) — it states required subjects, language versions, and any score policy.
3. For scholarships: confirm with **campuschina.org** (Chinese Government Scholarship) and the local Chinese embassy.
4. For domestic assessments: consult provincial education-examination authority (省教育考试院) sites; never compare gaokao scores across provinces.
5. For international benchmarking: use OECD (PISA) and IEA (TIMSS) primary reports, noting China's B-S-J-Z sampling caveat ([3.5](#35-international-assessments-pisa-and-timss)).

## 7. Glossary

- **3+1+2** — The new-gaokao model: 3 common subjects (Chinese, math, foreign language) + 1 preferred (physics or history) + 2 of 4 re-selected (chemistry, biology, politics, geography) ([3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination)).
- **3+X** — The older gaokao model: 3 common subjects + comprehensive arts/science paper; still used in the last unconverted provinces ([3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination)).
- **B-S-J-Z** — Beijing-Shanghai-Jiangsu-Zhejiang: the four regions making up China's PISA sample ([3.5](#35-international-assessments-pisa-and-timss)).
- **CGS (Chinese Government Scholarship)** — The flagship scholarship program administered by CSC for international students ([2.1](#21-purpose-and-policy-rationale)).
- **CSC (China Scholarship Council)** — 国家留学基金管理委员会; the agency that organizes CSCA and administers CGS ([1.1](#11-the-short-answer)).
- **CSCA** — China Scholastic Competency Assessment / 来华留学本科入学学业水平测试 ([1.3](#13-the-chinese-name-and-the-naming-problem)).
- **Gaokao 高考** — National College Entrance Examination ([3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination)).
- **HSK** — 汉语水平考试, the standard Chinese-language proficiency test ([4.3](#43-csca-vs-hsk-related-but-different)).
- **来华留学 (Láihuá liúxué)** — "Study in China"; the international-student track in Chinese higher education ([1.4](#14-how-csca-maps-to-chinas-assessment-landscape)).
- **强基计划 (Qiángjī jìhuà)** — Strong Foundation Plan: basic-discipline admissions channel combining gaokao and university assessment ([3.1](#31-gaokao--the-national-college-entrance-examination)).
- **双减 (Shuāngjiǎn)** — "Double Reduction" (2021): the policy cutting homework and banning for-profit K-9 tutoring ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams)).
- **新课标 (Xīn kèbiāo)** — the 2022 compulsory-education curriculum standards ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams)).
- **学业水平考试 (Xuéyè shuǐpíng kǎoshì)** — High-school academic proficiency exams: graduation requirement + selective-subject scores for the new gaokao ([3.2](#32--academic-proficiency-exams)).
- **Zhongkao 中考** — Senior-high entrance examination, city-administered ([3.4](#34-zhongkao--the-senior-high-entrance-exam)).
- **综合素质评价 (Zōnghé sùzhì píngjià)** — Comprehensive quality evaluation: the 德智体美劳 (morality, academics, physical health, aesthetics, labor) record used in admissions ([3.3](#33--comprehensive-quality-evaluation)).
